*ARTICLE 68.* Penalty to be imposed upon a person under eighteen years of age. - When the offender is a minor under eighteen years and his case is one coming under the provisions of the paragraphs next to the last of Article 80 of this Code, the following rules shall be observed:
    1. Upon a person under fifteen but over nine years of age, who is not exempted from liability by reason of the court having declared that he acted with discernment, a discretionary penalty shall be imposed, but always lower by two degrees at least than that prescribed by law for the crime which he committed.
    2. Upon a person over fifteen and under eighteen years of age the penalty next lower than that prescribed by law shall be imposed, but always in the proper period.

The second mitigating circumstance is "That the offender is under eighteen year of age or over seventy years."  
Minority, if not an exempting circumstance, is always a privileged mitigating circumstance. Minority is considered a privileged mitigating circumstance when the child in conflict with the law is over 15 but under 18 and he acted with discernment. It is always a privileged mitigating circumstance since it is expressly provided under second paragraph of Art. 68 of the RPC. The moment the offender is over 15 but under 18, the penalty shall be lowered by one (1) degree. Hence, it is a privileged mitigating circumstance, never an ordinary mitigating circumstance.  
X was borrowing money from Y, but the latter refused. X got mad and stabbed Y causing his death. At the time of the killing, X was 74 years old. X, being beyond 70 years old, such circumstance shall be considered as an ordinary mitigating circumstance. It is ordinary only because of RA 9346. 
Under RA 9346, if the crime is punished by death penalty, the penalty shall be automatically adjusted to reclusion perpetua. Therefore, regardless of whether the offender is 70 years old or not, if the crime committed is punishable by death, it shall be automatically reduced to reclusion perpetua not because of the offender's senior age but because of RA 9346.
