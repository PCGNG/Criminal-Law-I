Mitigating circumstances are those which, when present or attendant in the commission of the felony, would serve to lower, reduce, or decrease the imposable penalty.  
The penalty will be lowered because there is a diminution of the offender's voluntariness in the commission of the crime. There is either a diminished criminal intent, a diminished criminal action, or a diminished intelligence on the part of the accused.  
In exempting circumstances, there is a total lack of voluntariness which is why the offender is exempted from criminal liability. In mitigating circumstances, there is voluntariness, but it is diminished. Thus, its only effect is to reduce or decrease the imposable penalty.
*Kinds of Mitigating Circumstances*
There are two kinds of mitigating circumstances:  
      1. Ordinary mitigating circumstances, and 
      2. Privileged mitigating circumstances.

*ORDINARY MITIGATING CIRCUMSTANCES*
Ordinary mitigating circumstances may be offset by a generic aggravating circumstance. If it is not offset, its effect is to lower the  penalty  to the minimum period prescribed by law.    
*PRIVILEGED MITIGATING CIRCUMSTANCES*
Privileged mitigating circumstances cannot be offset by any kind of aggravating circumstance. Its effect is to lower the imposable penalty by one degree.
