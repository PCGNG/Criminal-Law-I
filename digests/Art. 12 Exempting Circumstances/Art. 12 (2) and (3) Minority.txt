### MINORITY

### RA 9344, AS AMENDED JUVENILE JUSTICE AND WELFARE ACT
### SECTION 6. 
Minimum Age of Criminal Responsibility. – A child fifteen (15) years of age or under at the time of the commission of the offense shall be exempt from criminal liability. However, the child shall be subjected to an intervention program pursuant to Sec. 20 of this Act.
A child is deemed to be fifteen (15) years of age on the day of the fifteenth anniversary of his/her birthdate. 
A child above fifteen (15) years but below eighteen (18) years of age shall likewise be exempt from criminal liability and be subjected to an intervention program, unless he/she has acted with discernment, in which case, such child shall be subjected to the appropriate proceedings in accordance with this Act. 
The exemption from criminal liability herein established does not include exemption from civil liability, which shall be enforced in accordance with existing laws.

### QUESTION: 
What is the effect of minority on the criminal liability of an offender? 

### ANSWER: 
If the question is vague, you have to qualify your answer based on the age of the child in conflict with the law. As provided for under Sec. 6 of RA 9344, as amended, or “The Juvenile Justice Act and Welfare Act”, a child 15 years old or under at the time of the commission of the crime shall be totally exempted from criminal liability regardless of discernment. If the child is above 15 but below 18 years old, he shall still be exempted from criminal liability unless he acted with discernment – in which case, he shall be prosecuted in court. Therefore, you have to first determine based on the facts if this minor child acted with discernment or not.

### NOTE: 
Under Sec. 6 of RA 9344, as amended, a child in conflict with the law shall only be exempted from criminal liability but not from civil liability because a wrong has indeed been committed – that is why it is an exempting circumstance and not a justifying circumstance.

### QUESTION: 
Who do you consider as a child in conflict with the law? 

### ANSWER: 
As defined under RA 9344, as amended, a child in conflict with the law is a child who alleged as, accused of, or adjudged as having committed an offense under Philippine Laws.

### SECTION 64. 
Children in Conflict with the Law Fifteen (15) Years Old and Below. – Upon effectivity of this Act, cases of children fifteen (15) years old and below at the time of the commission of the crime shall immediately be dismissed and the child shall be referred to the appropriate local social welfare and development officer. Such officer, upon thorough assessment of the child, shall determine whether to release the child to the custody of his/her parents, or refer the child to prevention programs as provided under this Act. Those with suspended sentences and undergoing rehabilitation at the youth rehabilitation center shall likewise be released, unless it is contrary to the best interest of the child.

### QUESTION: 
For the longest time, Brothers X and Y have been asking Z to join their Rugby Boys Gang, but Z would refuse. As a result, Brothers X and Y have these feelings against Z. 
X and Y are on one side of the road. Upon seeing Z coming, X and Y blocked the path of Z and told him, “Z, for the nth time, we invite you to join our Rugby Boys Gang.” However, Z answered, “X and Y, for the nth time, I do not want to be part of your gang.” Upon hearing these words, X and Y were very mad, and repeatedly boxed and mauled Z. For every attack, Z would bleed because the fists of X and Y have brass knuckles. Z fell on the ground. 
The cousin of Z begged X and Y to stop attacking Z, otherwise, Z might die. Instead of stopping, X went on the back of Z while Y went on the legs of Z. Thereafter, X strangled the neck of Z resulting to his death. After killing Z and before leaving, X and Y warned the cousin of Z, “If you tell anyone what we did, we will come after you and we will do to you what we did to him.” 
At the time of the commission of the crime, X was 14 years old, while Y was 16 years old. The cousin informed the authorities. Hence, X and Y were arrested. They were charged with the crime of Homicide for killing Z. 
If you are the judge, how will you rule on the said case?

### ANSWER:
In so far as X is concerned, X was only 14 years old at the time of the commission of the crime. Thus, the case should be immediately DISMISSED regardless of discernment.
Under Sec. 64 of RA 9344, as amended, the said law categorically provides that in case of children 15 years old and below at the time of the commission of the crime, the case shall be immediately dismissed.

### QUESTION: 
Did Y act with discernment such that he can be prosecuted in court or should the case against him be dismissed because he acted without discernment?

### ANSWER:
In so far as Y is concerned, Y was 16 years old at the time of the commission of the crime. Y falls under the category of above 15 but below 18 years old. Hence, discernment matters.
Y acted with discernment. Such discernment is evident from the fact that Y warned the cousin – which means Y has knowledge of the consequences of his act. He knew that killing Z is an unlawful act and that if the authorities discover such, he will be arrested and prosecuted. Thus, the case should PROCEED before the court, and he shall be charged with the crime of Homicide.

###SECTION 38. 
Automatic Suspension of Sentence. – Once the child who is under eighteen (18) years of age at the time of the commission of the offense is found guilty of the offense charged, the court shall determine and ascertain any civil liability which may have resulted from the offense committed. However, instead of pronouncing the judgment of conviction, the court shall place the child in conflict with the law under suspended sentence, without need of application: Provided, however, That suspension of sentence shall still be applied even if the juvenile is already eighteen years (18) of age or more at the time of the pronouncement of his/her guilt.
Upon suspension of sentence and after considering the various circumstances of the child, the court shall impose the appropriate disposition measures as provided in the SC Rule on Juveniles in Conflict with the Law.

### QUESTION:
Y was charged with Homicide before the RTC. On the basis of the right to speedy trial, the hearing for the case proceeded until its termination when Y was 17 years old. Y was still a minor at the time of the promulgation of judgment finding Y guilty beyond reasonable doubt. What is the duty of the court?

### ANSWER:
Under Sec. 38 of RA 9344, as amended, when a child in conflict with the law who is under 18 years old at the time of the commission of the crime is found guilty of the crime charged, the court shall determine and ascertain his civil liability arising from the crime committed. However, instead of pronouncing the judgment of conviction, the court shall place the child in conflict with the law under suspended sentence without need of application. Therefore, since the judge found Y guilty beyond reasonable doubt of Homicide at the time when he was CRIMINAL LAW REVIEW | 4D UST FCL | 2024 67 still a minor – 17 years old, then the judge shall motu proprio place Y under suspended sentence without need of application. There is no need for his counsel to file a motion for a suspended sentence.

### QUESTION:
Y was charged with the crime of Homicide. The trial of the case terminated when Y was already 20 years old. What is the duty of the court?
### ANSWER:
The court shall place Y under suspended sentence without the need of application because under Sec. 38 of RA 9344, as amended, the last sentence further provides that this suspended sentence will apply even if the juvenile offender is already 18 years old or above at the time of the promulgation of judgment. Even if the said child in conflict with the law who committed a crime when he was a minor, and became an adult at the time of the promulgation of judgment, such minor offender shall still be entitled to suspended sentence without need of application.

### SECTION 40. 
Return of the Child in Conflict with the Law to Court. – If the court finds that the objective of the disposition measures imposed upon the child in conflict with the law have not been fulfilled, or if the child in conflict with the law has willfully failed to comply with the conditions of his/her disposition or rehabilitation program, the child in conflict with the law shall be brought before the court for execution of judgment.
If said child in conflict with the law has reached eighteen (18) years of age while under suspended sentence, the court shall determine whether to discharge the child in accordance with this Act, to order execution of sentence, or to extend the suspended sentence for a certain specified period or until the child reaches the maximum age of twenty-one (21) years.

### QUESTION:
Y was charged with the crime of Homicide. Since he acted with discernment, trial on the merits ensued until its termination at the promulgation of judgment when Y was already 23 years old. Can the judge place him under suspended sentence?
### ANSWER:
NO. As provided for under Sec. 40 of RA 9344, as amended, the suspended sentence can be extended only until the child in conflict with the law reaches the maximum age of 21 years old. Since Y was already 23 years old, then he is no longer entitled to a suspended sentence. Therefore, he shall be convicted as charged, but his minority at the time of the commission of the crime will be considered privileged mitigating circumstance.
### NOTE:
If minority is not exempting, minority shall always be a privileged mitigating circumstance because of Art. 68 of the RPC, if the offender is a minor who acted with discernment, such minority at the time of the commission of the crime shall lower the imposable penalty by one degree.

### PEOPLE v. SARCIA G.R. NO. 169641 | SEPTEMBER 10, 2009 | LEONARDO-DE CASTRO, J.
### FACTS: 
AAA was playing with her cousin in the yard of Crisologo. Sarcia approached AAA and invited her to go with him. Unknown to Sarcia, the cousin of AAA followed them. Upon reaching the place, Sarcia removed AAA’s shorts and underwear. He also removed his trousers and brief. Thereafter, he ordered AAA to lie down on her back. He then laid on top of her and inserted his penis into AAA’s private organ. The cousin of AAA witnessed Sarcia’s dastardly act. He was charged with the crime of Rape. In his defense, he interposed that he was a minor at the time of the commission of the crime.

###ISSUE: 
W/N minority should be appreciated to exempt Sarcia from criminal liability (NO)

### RULING: 
NO. When accused appellant testified on March 14, 2002, he admitted that he was 24 years old, which means that in 1996, he was 18 years of age. As found by the trial court, the rape incident could have taken place "in any month and date in the year 1996." Since the prosecution was not able to prove the exact date and time when the rape was committed, it is not certain that the crime of rape was committed on or after he reached 18 years of age in 1996. In assessing the attendance of the mitigating circumstance of minority, all doubts should be resolved in favor of the accused, it being more beneficial to the latter. In fact, in several cases, this Court has appreciated this circumstance on the basis of a lone declaration of the accused regarding his age. Under Art. 68 of the RPC, when the offender is a minor under 18 years, the penalty next lower than that prescribed by law shall be imposed, but always in the proper period.

### PEOPLE v. MANTALABA G.R. NO. 186227 | JULY 20, 2011 | PERALTA, J.
### FACTS: 
The Task Force Regional Anti-Crime Emergency Response received a tip from an informant that Allen Mantalaba was selling Shabu. After forming a buy-bust team, a buy-bust operation was then conducted. After the exchange of a sachet of Shabu and marked money, the Mantalaba was arrested. He was also found in possession of a big sachet of Shabu. He was charged with the crime of Sale of Dangerous Drugs and Possession of Dangerous Drugs under RA 9165. In his defense, he interposed that he was a minor at the time of the commission of the crime. 

### ISSUE: 
W/N Mantalaba is entitled to suspended sentence on the ground of minority (NO) 

### RULING: 
NO. While Sec. 38 of RA 9344 provides that suspension of sentence can still be applied even if the child in conflict with the law is already eighteen (18) years of age or more at the time of the pronouncement of his/her guilt, Sec. 40 of the same law limits the said suspension of sentence until the child reaches the maximum age of 21. In the present case, Allen, who is now beyond the age of twenty-one (21) years can no longer avail of the provisions of Sections 38 and 40 of RA 9344 as to his suspension of sentence, because such is already moot and academic.

### QUESTION:
In the cases of People v. Sarcia and People v. Mantalaba which were assigned to you: What if the case filed against a minor offender is a heinous crime? Let say the case filed against a minor offender is Qualified Rape or Murder. These are heinous crimes punishable by Reclusion Perpetua or death. Can he still be given suspended sentence as provided for under Sec. 38 of RA 9344, as amended?
### ANSWER:
YES. The SC in the cases of People v. Sarcia and People v. Mantalaba said Sec. 38 of RA 9344, as amended, does not distinguish as to the crime committed by a child in conflict with the law. Whether the crime committed is a heinous crime punishable by Reclusion Perpetua or death, or a crime of lesser penalty, it is the duty of the court to place the said child in conflict with the law under suspended sentence without the need of application.
If the law does not distinguish, neither does the court distinguish. Hence, regardless of the crime committed by the said child in conflict with the law, he is entitled to a suspended sentence without the need of application, provided that at the time of the promulgation of judgment, he is not yet beyond 21 years old. 
The moment the said offender, although a minor at the time of the commission of the crime, was already beyond 21 years old at the time of the promulgation of judgment, the court shall render the judgment of conviction. However, the penalty to be imposed shall consider the privileged mitigating circumstance of minority based on Article 68, second paragraph of the RPC. 
Likewise, based on Sec. 51 of RA 9344, as amended, in lieu of the confinement in a regular penal institution, this offended who was a minor at the time of the commission of the crime shall serve his sentence in agricultural camps and other training facilities that may be established, maintained, supervised, and controlled by the Bureau of Correction in coordination with the DSWD. 
Therefore, for as long as the offender is a minor at the time of the commission of the crime, regardless of his age at the time of promulgation of the judgment, he cannot be placed together with hardened criminals in regular penal institutions. 
In the En banc case of People v. Gambao, the SC held that in so far as the application of Art. 51, the age of the child in conflict with the law at the time of the promulgation of judgment is immaterial. What matters is the age of the offender at the time of the commission of the crime. NOTE: For as long as the offender is a minor at the time of the commission of the crime, in case of conviction, he shall not be made to serve his sentence in regular penal institutions.

### PEOPLE v. ZZZ G.R. NO. 228828 | JULY 24, 2019 | LEONEN, J.
### FACTS: 
The uncle of the victim testified that when he was on his way to the store to buy cigarettes, he suddenly saw ZZZ dragging the victim by the wrists towards a school. Days later, the cadaver of the victim was found in a bamboo grove. The autopsy report showed visible signs of sexual assault and a cracked skull. ZZZ was charged with the crime of Rape with Homicide. In his defense, he interposed that he was a minor at the time of the commission of the crime.
### ISSUE: 
W/N ZZZ acted with discernment (YES)
### RULING: 
YES. Under Sec. 6 of the RA 9344, a child above 15 years old but below 18 years old is not exempt from criminal liability when the child acted with discernment. In this case, accused-appellant acted with discernment in carrying out the crime – (i) He perpetrated the crime in a dark and isolated place; (ii) After knowing that he had been tagged as the suspect, he evaded authorities by fleeing to Tarlac and concealing his identity; (iii) As confirmed by the social worker assigned to him, he knew and understood the consequences of his acts; and (iv) Dr. Bandonill concluded that AAA was raped by means of force, as evidenced by the contusions all over her body and by the tear from her vaginal area.
### NOTE: 
In the case of People v. ZZZ, the SC has defined discernment as the mental capacity of a minor to fully appreciate the consequences of his unlawful act.

### SECTION 57. 
Status Offenses. – Any conduct not considered an offense or not penalized if committed by an adult shall not be considered an offense and shall not be punished if committed by a child." 
### SECTION 57-A. 
Violations of Local Ordinances. – Ordinances enacted by local governments concerning juvenile status offenses such as, but not limited to, curfew violations, truancy, parental disobedience, anti smoking and anti-drinking laws, as well as light offenses and misdemeanors against public order or safety such as, but not limited to, disorderly conduct, public scandal, harassment, drunkenness, public intoxication, criminal nuisance, vandalism, gambling, mendicancy, littering, public urination, and trespassing, shall be for the protection of children. No penalty shall be imposed on children for said violations, and they shall instead be brought to their residence or to any barangay official at the barangay hall to be released to the custody of their parents. Appropriate intervention programs shall be provided for in such ordinances. The child shall also be recorded as a ‘child at risk’ and not as a ‘child in conflict with the law’. The ordinance shall also provide for intervention programs, such as counseling, attendance in group activities for children, and for the parents, attendance in parenting education seminars.

### QUESTION:
What are status offenses?
### ANSWER:
Under Sec. 57 of RA 9344, as amended by RA 10630, status offense refers to any conduct not considered an offense or not penalized if committed by a child. These status offenses include curfew violations, truancy, parental disobedience, anti-smoking, anti-drinking, etc.
### QUESTION:
Can LGUs enact or pass ordinances on status offenses?
### ANSWER:
YES. Sec. 57 of RA 9344 was amended by RA 10630. There is a new Sec. 57-A which provides that LGUs are allowed to enact or pass ordinances on status offenses, provided they shall be intended for the protection of minors or children. Therefore, no penalty shall be imposed on children who violate the said ordinances; instead, these minors or children shall be brought to their residence, or to the barangay hall to be released to the custody of their parents only. Likewise, under Sec. 57-A of RA 9344 was amended by RA 10630, the said minors shall not be called as children in conflict with the law; instead, they shall be called as children at risk.

### SAMAHAN NG MGA PROGRESIBONG KABATAAN v. QUEZON CITY G.R. NO. 225442 | AUGUST 8, 2017 | PERLAS BERNABE, J.
### FACTS: 
Following the campaign of President Duterte to implement a nationwide curfew for minors, several local governments in Metro Manila started to strictly implement their curfew ordinances on minors through police operations. Samahan ng mga Progresibong Kabataan (SPARK) filed a Petition assailing the constitutionality of the Curfew Ordinances of Quezon City, Manila, and Navotas on the ground that the imposition of the penalties thereof contravenes RA 9344 which provides that no penalty shall be imposed on minors for curfew violations. 
### ISSUE: 
W/N Sec. 4 of the Manila Curfew Ordinance contravenes Sec. 57-A of R.A. 9344 (YES) 
### RULING: 
YES. The provisions of RA 9344 provisions do not prohibit the enactment of regulations that curtail the conduct of minors, when the similar conduct of adults are not considered as an offense or penalized (i.e., status offenses). Instead, what they prohibit is the imposition of penalties on minors for violations of these regulations. Consequently, the enactment of curfew ordinances on minors, without penalizing them for violations thereof, is not violative of Section 57-A. The provisions of RA 9344, as amended, should not be read to mean that all the actions of the minor in violation of the regulations are without legal consequences. Section 57-A thereof empowers local governments to adopt appropriate intervention programs, such as community-based programs recognized under Section 54 of the same law.
For imposing the sanctions of reprimand, fine, and/or imprisonment on minors for curfew violations, portions of Section 4 of the Manila Ordinance directly and irreconcilably conflict with the clear language of Section 57-A of RA 9344, as amended, and is invalid

### NOTE: 
In the case of Samahan ng mga Progresibong Kabataan v. Quezon City, here comes the Samahan which questioned the ordinances of Quezon City, Manila and Navotas. The curfew ordinance of Quezon City was declared constitutional by the SC, but the curfew ordinances of Manila and Navotas were declared unconstitutional because penalties were imposed on minors for being arrested in violation of the said ordinance; therefore, null and void. However, in so far as the Quezon City ordinance is concerned, there was no penalty imposed on minors; instead, it will be the parents.
