Art. 12 (1) Imbecile or Insane Person

 
Under the first paragraph of Art. 12, we have two (2) exempting circumstances: 
 
1. Insanity, and  
2. Imbecility.  

INSANITY
- An insane person is someone who lacks total mental aberration of the mind. That insane person cannot distinguish right from wrong, good from evil.
- Insanity is not exempting in all circumstances. It is necessary that it must be proven that the allegedly insane person did not act during lucid interval, otherwise, he can still be convicted.  

IMBECILITY
- An imbecile person is someone who is already advanced in age, but he has only the mental capacity of a 2 to 7-year old child. 
- Imbecility is exempting under any and all circumstances. There is no lucid interval in so far as an imbecile person is concerned.  


The law, particularly, the Civil Code, presumes every man is sane, that every man is of sound mind. The law presumes that all acts done by a man are voluntary and willful. Therefore, based on this, an offender or an accused, who pleads the exempting circumstance of insanity, has the burden of proving beyond reasonable doubt that he or she was insane immediately, prior to, or
at the time of the commission of the crime.  

 
If the offender was able to prove that he was insane immediately prior to, or at the time of the commission of the crime, then he shall be absolved of criminal liability because an insane person has no full and clear understanding of the nature and consequences of his act. However, if the said insane person failed to prove that he was insane immediately prior to, or at the time of the commission of the crime, then he shall be convicted as charged.  

If the said person was sane at the time of the commission of the crime, he only became insane while his case was ongoing. Therefore, he only became insane after the commission of the crime. Such kind of insanity will not free him from criminal liability because the insanity that is exempting must be present immediately prior to, or at the time of the commission of the crime.

 
If the insanity existed after the commission of the crime, the said offender shall only be brought to the mental institution. His case shall first be archived, then the hearing of the case will be suspended. The moment he regains sanity, he will again be prosecuted before the court except when the crime has already prescribed.  

### QUESTION
 X was awakened at 5 o' clock in the morning. Upon getting out of bed, X immediately went to the kitchen. X took a knife and went to the house of their neighbor Y. He forcibly entered into the house of Y and went directly inside the bedroom. Finding Y asleep, X repeatedly stabbed Y until Y died. While still holding the weapon, X got out of the house of Y and went to their house. He saw his sister. The sister asked X what happened. X gave the knife to his sister and the sister embraced X. Thereafter, X sat on the ground, stomping his feet, and crying aloud like a child. The relatives of Y saw the incident and called the police. The police arrived and invited X for questioning at the police station. 

 
X is now being prosecuted for the crime of Murder. According to X, he should not be held criminally liable because immediately prior to and at the time of the commission of the crime, he was suffering from insanity. Hence, X raised the defense of insanity.

 
To prove insanity, the defense counsel presented X himself. He testified in court that prior to the day of killing Y, he was suffering from all kinds of anxiety, sleepless nights, lack of appetite and nervousness. According to X, there was this voice ringing in his head saying that he has to kill Y and he only obeyed the said voice.  


After X testified in court, the defense counsel presented the sister. The sister testified in court that prior to the day of the killing, she would often see her brother looking blankly at nowhere and talking to no one. There was also a time that she saw her brother hitting himself on the day of the commission of the crime. According to the sister, her brother went home 
crying and gave her the knife. Thereafter, her brother sat on the ground and cried aloud like a child. She said her brother is insane immediately prior to, or at the time of the commission of the crime.  

 
Is X’s defense of insanity present so as to free him from criminal liability? Will the defense of insanity lie in his favor? 

### ANSWER

 NO. The SC said that, in one of the cases assigned to you and other similar cases, even assuming that the accused's testimony is credible, these are merely suggestive of an abnormal mental condition which cannot be equated with total deprivation of will and total power to discern. Mere abnormality of mental faculties will not exclude imputability. Therefore, his defense of insanity will not lie in his favor.  

### TURALBA v. PEOPLE G.R. NO. 216453 | MARCH 16, 2022 | LOPEZ, M., J. 

### FACTS: 
Gregorio was driving his Honda CRV to buy some bread. When he arrived, he parked his CRV ten (10) meters away across the bakery and left the car key inside the vehicle. He proceeded to the bakery and bought bread. However, when he turned around to check on his car, he noticed that his car was already  moving towards another street. It was being driven by Oligario Turalba. Gregorio immediately flagged down and boarded a tricycle to pursue his car. The chase ended when the car was caught on a traffic congestion. Gregorio was able to recover his car and Turalba was later apprehended. Turalba was charged with the crime of Carnapping. In his defense, he interposed that he was suffering from psychosis. 

### ISSUE: 
W/N insanity should be appreciated (NO) 

### RULING: 
NO. As a defense, insanity is in the nature of a confession and avoidance. The person who asserts insanity is, in effect, admitting to the commission of the crime. Consequently, the burden of proof shifts to him, who must prove his defense with clear and convincing evidence. Differently stated, after a plea of insanity, the accused is tried on the issue of sanity alone, and if found to be sane, a judgment of conviction is rendered without any trial on the issue of guilt, because the accused had already admitted committing the crime. 

Insanity is not easily vailable to the accused as a successful defense. It is an exception rather than the rule on the human condition. Insanity as a condition of the mind, is not susceptible of the usual means of proof as no man can know what is going on in the mind of another, the state of condition of a person's mind can only be measured and judged by behavior. Thus, the accused must prove the following: first, that the insanity constitutes a complete deprivation of intelligence, reason, or discernment; and second, the insanity existed at the time of, or immediately preceding, the commission of the crime. 

To establish insanity, opinion testimony is required which may be given by a witness who is intimately acquainted with the accused, has rational basis to conclude that the accused was insane based on his own perception, or is qualified as an expert, such as a psychiatrist. The Court stress that an inquiry into the mental state of an accused should relate to the period 
immediately before or at the very moment the felony is committed. 

In the case at bar, no witness was presented by the petitioner to show that he exhibited any symptoms associated with psychosis immediately before or simultaneous with the carnapping incident. The record is bereft of even a single account of abnormal or bizarre behavior on the part of the petitioner prior to November 20, 2007. Although Dr. Evangelista opined that he is 
suffering from psychosis, she declared that it is difficult to assess the exact mental condition of Oligario, having seen the latter only once, and she could not even identify the kind of psychosis is afflicted with. 

Likewise, no evidence was presented to show proof of abnormal behavior immediately before or simultaneous to the commission of the crime. While it can be true that there was some impairment of the petitioner’s mental faculties, since he was said to suffer from psychosis, the Court held that such impairment was not so complete as to deprive him of his intelligence or the consciousness of his acts. 

### NOTE

 In that case, accused Turalba was charged with the crime of Carnapping. The defense raised by Turalba was insanity. To prove Turalba’s insanity at the time of the commission of the crime, the defense counsel presented the doctor. According to the doctor, she first met Turalba at the mental institution in Mariveles and based on his diagnosis of Turalba, Turalba has psychosis or “nawawala sa sarili” because of his continuous use of alcohol and Shabu or drugs.  

 
Will such defense be sufficient in order to prove insanity for the crime of kidnapping? NO. The SC convicted Turalba. Although it may be true that by reason of alcoholism and use of drugs which resulted in psychosis, the accused has suffered impairment of mental faculties, such impairment does not mean complete deprivation of his intelligence and of his consciousness in the doing of his act. Hence, such defense of insanity will not lie in his favor.
