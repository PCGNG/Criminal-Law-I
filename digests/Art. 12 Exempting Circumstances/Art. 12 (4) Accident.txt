### 4. ACCIDENT
Elements of Art. 12(4) – Accident
Accident as an exempting circumstance has the following elements:
1. That the offender is performing a lawful act; 
2. That he was performing the lawful act with due care; 
3. That he caused an injury by mere accident; and 
4. That said injury was caused without fault or intent on the part of the offender.
Accident is an exception to the general rule that in case of exempting circumstances, there is no criminal liability but there is civil liability. If accident is present in the commission of a crime, there is no criminal liability nor civil liability.

### PEOPLE v. LEOCADIO G.R. NO. 227396 | FEBRUARY, 22, 2023 | LOPEZ M., J.
### FACTS:
One afternoon, the parents of AAA337396 instructed her and her two brothers to collect the payment of rice cakes from their neighbor, Leocadio. However, AAA337689 failed to return home. Her parents searched for her the whole night, but to no avail. The following day, the lifeless body of AAA337689 was found underneath Leocadio's wooden bed, lying flat on her back with a cloth wrapped around her mouth and nose, and with both hands tied and twisted at her back. She died from asphyxia by suffocation. Further, the medical examiner revealed that she also sustained hymenal lacerations, and injuries and abrasions on her body. Leocadio was charged with the complex crime of Rape with Homicide. In his defense, he interposed accident.
### ISSUE:
W/N accident should be appreciated (NO)
### RULING:
NO. Accident is an affirmative defense in which the accused is burdened to establish with clear and convincing proof. It is incumbent upon him to prove the presence of the requisites of accident as an exempting circumstance, as follows: (i) a person is performing a lawful act; (ii) with due care; (iii) he/she causes an injury to another by mere accident; and (iv) without fault or intention of causing it.
In the present case, the exempting circumstance of accident is inapplicable.
As to the first requisite, the SC held that it is absent. Milo's act of boxing AAA227396 on the chest is unlawful, constituting at least the felony of physical injuries.
For the second requisite, it is likewise lacking. Leocadio acted without due care and prudence as his supposed unconscious punching of the victim amounts to a negligent act absent evidence that he was totally deprived of his powers of observation and mental faculties. It is preposterous to believe that the initial reaction of a person who was suddenly awakened is to indiscriminately throw punches.
As to the third and fourth requisite, the Court found that Leocadio has intent to kill. It must be noted that AAA227396 died due to asphyxia by suffocation and was found underneath Milo's bed with a cloth wrapped around her mouth and nose, and with both hands tied and twisted at her back. Further, she also sustained multiple injuries and abrasions. The report also supports the conclusion that the injuries were inflicted to repel any resistance that AAA227396 may offer Leocadio.
### NOTE: 
In the case of People vs. Leocadio, penned by your Bar chair Justice Mario Lopez, the accused was charged with the special complex crime of Rape with Homicide. The defense raised by the accused was accident. According to him, he was startled when the victim tried to awaken him which made him box the chest of the victim. The SC held that the defense raised by the accused is inapplicable. One does not raise the defense of accident in the special complex crime of Rape with Homicide. The SC gave two reasons:
First, the first element of accident requires that the act done must be lawful. Granting for the sake of argument that the act done by the accused was only to punch the chest of the girl, the same is still unlawful because it amounts to physical injury. 
Second, since the victim died, intent to kill becomes a general criminal intent which is presumed. Thus, the SC held that the defense of accident is inapplicable. 
Therefore, before accident may be raised by the accused to exempt himself from criminal liability, it is necessary that first and foremost he was performing a lawful act.
