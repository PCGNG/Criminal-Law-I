### 5. IRRESISTIBLE FORCE 
Elements of Art. 12(5) – Irresistible Force 
1. The force must be a physical force; 
2. The said physical force must be irresistible; and 
3. The physical force must come from a third person.
It is necessary that the force employed by the third person on the accused must be irresistible such that it reduced him to a mere instrument that he acted not only without will but also against his will. Totally, there is no freedom of choice on the part of the accused. He has no choice but to commit the crime which he is being asked to do.

### 6. UNCONTROLLABLE FEAR
The sixth exempting circumstance – “Any person who acts under the impulse of an uncontrollable fear of an equal or greater injury.”
Elements of Art. 12(6) – Uncontrollable Fear 
1. There exists an uncontrollable fear;
2. The fear must be real or at least imminent; and 
3. The fear of the injury must be greater than or equal to the act committed. 
Just like the fifth exempting circumstance, the offender does not have freedom of action – an element of voluntariness. The said offender has no choice but to do the criminal act which he is required to do.

### QUESTION:
X is a victim of gang rape. She became pregnant. When she was three (3) months pregnant, she went to a doctor. She wanted the doctor to perform an abortion but the doctor refused, saying that his duty is to save lives and not to kill. After saying that, X took a bottle of Iodine from her bag and threatened the doctor that if he does not perform an abortion on her, she will drink the bottle of Iodine. The doctor took pity on her and thereafter, performed the abortion. Can the accused raise the exempting circumstance of having acted under the impulse of an uncontrollable fear of an equal or greater injury?
### ANSWER:
NO. Look at the facts and the phrase used by the Bar examiner. Taking pity on X, the doctor performed the abortion. To take pity on someone means to do an act voluntarily. Therefore, it cannot be said that in performing the abortion, the doctor acted under the impulse of an uncontrollable fear of an equal or greater injury. It cannot be said that he had no freedom of action or freedom of choice. Therefore, this exempting circumstance will not lie in his favor.

### QUESTION:
X was walking when he heard gun shots. Thinking he was the one being fired at, he sat on the ground. After several seconds, he realized he was not the one being shot at. He stood up and decided to proceed going home, but he heard laughter coming from the other side of the road.
X went to the place where the sound was coming from and there, he saw three (3) men whom he knew to be A, B, and C, and one man whom he knew to be Y. He saw that the three (3) men were all armed with guns and the victim Y was lying on the ground, bloody.
X then saw A firing at Y who was hit on the chest. X decided to leave but B and C saw him, and he was taken to A. X promised them that he will not tell anything to the authorities. A forced X to open his mouth and placed the gun in his mouth. Thereafter, A told X, “Shoot Y. If you do not shoot Y, I will shoot you right now.” Afraid that he will be killed, X shot Y who was hit on the head. Y died.
A, B, and C were arrested and they were charged with the crime of Murder but they all pointed to X as the one who inflicted the fatal blow. A, B, and C told the authorities that X was a co-conspirator. X was also arrested. X raised the exempting circumstance that he merely acted under the impulse of an uncontrollable fear of an equal or greater injury. Are all the elements present?
### ANSWER:
YES. All the elements are present. First element: There must exist an uncontrollable fear. What is that uncontrollable fear? X will be shot causing his death. Second element: The fear must be real or imminent. X saw A firing at Y with his own eyes. Definitely, A can also fire at him. Third element: The fear of the injury must be greater than or equal to the act committed. X feared being killed and this fear is greater than his fear of killing another person. Therefore, the exempting circumstance under the impulse of an uncontrollable fear may be appreciated in X’s favor.
