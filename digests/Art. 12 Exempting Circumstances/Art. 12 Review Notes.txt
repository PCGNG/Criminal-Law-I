Exempting circumstances are those circumstances which if present or attendant in the commission of a felony would serve to exempt the offender from criminal liability but not from civil liability.  
 
In case of justifying circumstances, as a rule, no criminal liability, no civil liability. In case of exempting circumstances, no criminal liability, but as a rule, there is civil liability because a wrong has indeed been committed.  
 
In exempting circumstances, the offender is absolved of criminal liability because the offender acted without voluntariness. There is the absence of any of the elements of voluntariness. We have discussed that the element of voluntariness, in case of intentional felony, we have:   
 
1. Criminal intent,  
2. Freedom of action, and
3. Intelligence on the part of the offender.  
 
While the elements of voluntariness, in case of culpable felony are: 
 
1. Criminal negligence,  
2. Freedom of action, and 
3. Intelligence on the part of the offender.  
 
In case of exempting circumstances, any of the element of voluntariness is absent. Therefore, since the offender acted without voluntariness, he is absolved of criminal liability but not of civil liability because a wrong, a violation of the law has indeed been committed.  
 
If an accused is charged in court and the said accused raised as a defense any of the exempting circumstances under Art.12, the said offender is indeed admitting the acts alleged in the Information, but he is saying that he is absolved of criminal liability because he acted without criminal voluntariness.  
 
There is already absence in criminal intent, freedom of action, or intelligence on his part. He will be moving for an acquittal – he will be moving that he be absolved for criminal liability. Therefore, to raise an exempting circumstance is also an act of admission and an act of avoidance. The offender, in effect, admits the acts alleged in the Information but he is trying to avoid criminal responsibility.
