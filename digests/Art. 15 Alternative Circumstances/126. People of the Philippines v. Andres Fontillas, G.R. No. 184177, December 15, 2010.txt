### **People of the Philippines v. Andres C. Fontillas alias "Anding"**

**G.R. No. 184177 | December 15, 2010 | Leonardo-De Castro, *J.***

---

#### Facts

Accused-appellant Andres C. Fontillas was charged before the Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Iba, Zambales, with the crime of qualified (incestuous) rape committed against his biological daughter, AAA, who was then 13 years old.

* **The Incident (December 8, 2001):** Around 11:00 p.m., AAA was sleeping with her younger brother BBB inside their shanty in Bamban, Masinloc, Zambales. She was awakened by her father's arrival and coughing. Seeing that he was drunk from a drinking spree with friends, AAA helped him enter and allowed him to lie down beside them.
* Sometime later, AAA was roused when appellant began removing her shorts. She cried, but appellant warned her not to make noise. After removing his own pants, appellant pinned her hands and feet (*inipit*), covered her mouth, and threatened that if she reported the incident, he would kill the person to whom she reported it. Overcome with fear, AAA could not resist as appellant inserted his penis into her vagina, causing her pain.
* After appellant left the following morning, AAA went to her Aunt CCC and Uncle DDD to report the rape. She was brought to the police station and subsequently placed under the protective custody of the DSWD Home for Girls in Olongapo City.
* **Medical Findings:** The medico-legal officer examined AAA on December 10, 2001 (two days later) and noted healed/"old" hymenal lacerations at the 6 and 8 o’clock positions, admitting one finger with ease.
* **Defense Theory:** Appellant raised denial and alibi, claiming he got excessively drunk during a drinking spree, could not recall what transpired, and only remembered waking up at 6:00 a.m. by the door. In the alternative, he argued that his extreme intoxication should at least mitigate his criminal liability.

The RTC convicted appellant of qualified rape and imposed the death penalty. On automatic review, the Court of Appeals affirmed the conviction but reduced the penalty to *reclusion perpetua* pursuant to Republic Act No. 9346.

---

#### Issues

1. Whether the prosecution proved accused-appellant's guilt beyond reasonable doubt despite his denial, the nature of the threat (not directed at the victim herself), and the presence of "old" hymenal lacerations.
2. Whether appellant’s alleged intoxication on the night of the incident should be appreciated as an alternative mitigating circumstance under Article 15 of the Revised Penal Code.

---

#### Ruling

##### **1. Guilt Proven Beyond Reasonable Doubt**

* **Positive and Credible Testimony:** AAA gave a candid, consistent, and straightforward account of how she was ravished by her own father. It is settled doctrine that when a woman, particularly a minor child, claims she was raped by her biological father, her testimony is entitled to full faith and credit, as no daughter would falsely subject herself and her father to such infamy absent any ill motive.
* **Nature of Threat and Intimidation:** The threat made by appellant—that he would kill the person to whom she would report the ordeal—was sufficient intimidation. It is of no moment that the death threat was not directed at AAA herself; it still instilled genuine terror that someone else would be harmed because of her. Furthermore, the moral ascendancy of a father over a 13-year-old child substitute for physical violence or intimidation in rape cases.
* **Hymenal Laceration Findings:** The presence of "old" hymenal lacerations did not weaken AAA's credibility. The medical examination took place two days after the assault, and lacerations in mucosal tissues can heal rapidly. Moreover, the crime of rape requires merely the slightest penetration of the female organ; a fresh hymenal tear is not an indispensable element.
* **Denial and Alibi:** Bare denial is a weak defense that cannot overcome positive, affirmative testimony from a credible witness.

---

##### RATIO

**2. Rejection of Intoxication as a Mitigating Circumstance**
ART. 15. Their concept. – Alternative circumstances are those which must be taken into consideration as aggravating or mitigating according to the nature and effects of the crime and the other conditions attending its commission. They are the relationship, intoxication and the degree of instruction and education of the offender.
x x x x
The intoxication of the offender shall be taken into consideration as a mitigating circumstance when the offender has committed a felony in a state of intoxication, if the same is not habitual or subsequent to the plan to commit said felony; but when the intoxication is habitual or intentional, it shall be considered as an aggravating circumstance.
Accused appellant did not present any evidence that his intoxication was not habitual or subsequent to the plan to commit the rape. The person pleading intoxication must likewise prove that he took such quantity of alcoholic beverage, prior to the commission of the crime, as would blur his reason.24 Accused-appellant utterly failed to present clear and convincing proof of the extent of his intoxication on the night of December 8, 2001 and that the amount of liquor he had taken was of such quantity as to affect his mental faculties. Not one of accused-appellant's drinking buddies testified that they, in fact, consumed eight bottles of gin prior to the rape incident.

---

#### **Key Doctrine**

> **Requisites for Intoxication as a Mitigating Circumstance (Art. 15, RPC):**
> *To be appreciated as a mitigating circumstance, the defense must establish with clear and convincing proof that: (1) the intoxication was not habitual; (2) it was not subsequent to the plan to commit the crime; and (3) the amount of alcohol consumed was sufficient to impair mental faculties and blur reason. Self-serving assertions of memory loss or drunkenness, without corroboration as to the quantity of liquor consumed and the absence of habituality, will not suffice.*
