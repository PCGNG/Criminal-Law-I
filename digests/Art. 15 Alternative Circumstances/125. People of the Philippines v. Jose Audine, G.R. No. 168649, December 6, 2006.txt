### **People of the Philippines v. Jose Alvizo Audine**

**G.R. No. 168649 | December 6, 2006 | Chico-Nazario, *J.***

---

#### Facts

Accused-appellant Jose Alvizo Audine was charged before the Regional Trial Court (RTC) of Marikina City with two counts of qualified rape committed against his minor biological daughter, AAA (14 years old at the time of the incidents).

* **First Incident (December 24, 1999):** In the early morning hours, appellant ordered AAA to go into their bedroom to fold clothes. Appellant followed her, boxed her abdomen when she resisted, forcibly stripped her, poked a small knife at her neck, and successfully had carnal knowledge of her.
* **Second Incident (January 8, 2000):** AAA was resting in the bedroom with her siblings when appellant entered, ordered the other children out to play, locked the door, and again raped AAA at knife-point.

As a consequence of the sexual abuses, AAA became pregnant and subsequently gave birth to a baby boy.

AAA initially kept silent due to fear and explicit death threats made by appellant against her and her siblings. In December 2000, noticing her physical changes, AAA's aunt questioned her, prompting AAA to reveal her ordeal. Accompanied by her aunt and a social worker from the DSWD, AAA filed complaints against her father.

In his defense, appellant interposed denial and alibi, claiming that on the dates of the alleged rapes, he was working at a construction site and could not have committed the acts charged.

The RTC convicted appellant of two counts of qualified rape and sentenced him to the supreme penalty of death for each count. The Court of Appeals affirmed the RTC’s judgment *in toto*.

---

#### Issues

1. Whether the prosecution established the guilt of the accused-appellant beyond reasonable doubt despite the victim's delay in reporting the rape and her initial lack of physical resistance.
2. What is the proper penalty imposable on appellant in light of Republic Act No. 9346 (An Act Prohibiting the Imposition of Death Penalty in the Philippines)?

---

#### Ruling

##### **1. Guilt Proven Beyond Reasonable Doubt**

The Supreme Court affirmed the conviction:

* **Victim's Credibility and Moral Ascendancy:** AAA gave a direct, candid, and unwavering account of her harrowing ordeal, which was corroborated by medical findings and the birth of her child. A young daughter would not subject herself to public humiliation and accuse her own biological father of a heinous offense unless it were the unvarnished truth.
* **Justified Delay in Reporting:** The delay in reporting an incestuous rape does not impair the victim's credibility. The moral ascendancy, parental authority, and intimidation exerted by a father—coupled with explicit death threats—sufficiently explain why a minor victim would be coerced into silence.
* **Defense of Alibi and Denial:** Bare denials and uncorroborated alibis cannot overcome the positive, categorical identification made by the victim.

##### **2. Imposable Penalty under R.A. No. 9346**

Under Articles 266-A and 266-B of the Revised Penal Code, the crime of rape committed by a father against his minor daughter with the use of a deadly weapon constitutes qualified rape punishable by death.

However, with the enactment and retroactivity of **Republic Act No. 9346** prohibiting the death penalty, the penalty of death was reduced to ***reclusion perpetua*** for each count, without eligibility for parole pursuant to Section 3 of R.A. No. 9346.

---
### Ratio
*The pertinent provisions of the Revised Penal Code relative to the case on hand are Articles 266-A and 266-B, which read:*
Art. 266-A. Rape; When and How Committed. - Rape is committed -  
  1) By a man who shall have carnal knowledge of a woman under any of the following circumstances:  
        a) Through force, threat, or intimidation;  xxXX  

Art. 266-B. Penalties. -  
XXXX  
The death penalty shall also be imposed if the crime of rape is committed with any of the following aggravating/qualifying circumstances:  
    1) When the victim is under eighteen (18) years of age and the offender is a parent, ascendant, step-parent, guardian, relative by consanguinity or affinity within the third civil degree, or the common-law spouse of the parent of the victim.  

For one to be convicted of qualified rape, at least one of the aggravating/qualifying circumstances mentioned in Article 266-B of the Revised Penal Code must be alleged in the information and duly proved during the trial.44 In the instant case, since the special qualifying circumstances of the victim's minority and her relationship with the offender have been properly alleged in the informations and established during trial, the imposition of the death penalty for each count of rape is justified.

---
#### Key Doctrine

> *In incestuous rape, delay in reporting the assault does not undermine the victim's credibility. The moral ascendancy exercised by a father over his minor child, combined with death threats, creates an atmosphere of fear and intimidation that fully explains the victim's reluctance and silence. Uncorroborated denials and alibi cannot stand against positive, credible identification by the victim.*
