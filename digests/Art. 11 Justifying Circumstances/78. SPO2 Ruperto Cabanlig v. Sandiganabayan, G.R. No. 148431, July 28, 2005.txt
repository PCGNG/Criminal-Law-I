### Facts
Petitioner SPO2 Ruperto Cabanlig, together with several fellow police officers (including SPO2 Pader y Paragas and SPO1 Padilla), was tasked with securing and escorting a notorious armed robbery suspect, Jimmy Padilla, during an ocular inspection/follow-up operation in Valenzuela City.

While the police team and the detainee were aboard an unmarked police service jeep, Jimmy Padilla suddenly lunged at escort officer SPO2 Pader. Jimmy grabbed Pader's service firearm (an M16 Armalite rifle) and grappled fiercely for its control. Pader shouted that Jimmy was grabbing his firearm ("Inaagaw ang baril ko!").

As Jimmy managed to twist and wrest the rifle toward the officers, petitioner Cabanlig, who was seated directly across, drew his .45 caliber service pistol and fired successive shots at Jimmy, hitting him. Jimmy fell mortally wounded and died shortly after arriving at the hospital.

An Information for Murder was filed against Cabanlig and his fellow officers before the Sandiganbayan. The Sandiganbayan convicted Cabanlig of Homicide, rejecting his invocation of both self-defense and the performance of a lawful duty under Article 11(5) of the Revised Penal Code, ruling that Cabanlig should have merely disabled Jimmy instead of firing lethal rounds into vital parts of his body.

### Issue
•  Whether petitioner SPO2 Ruperto Cabanlig is entitled to the justifying circumstance of lawful performance of duty under Article 11, paragraph 5 of the Revised Penal Code.
•  Whether petitioner is alternatively justified under self-defense (Article 11[1], RPC) and defense of a stranger/colleague (Article 11[3], RPC).

### Ruling
1. Yes. The petitioner acted in the lawful exercise of a right or in the proper performance of a duty under Article 11(5) of the RPC.
Under Article 11, paragraph 5 of the Revised Penal Code, an act is justified when done in the lawful exercise of a right or in the fulfillment of a duty or lawful office. Two requisites must concur:
•	That the accused acted in the performance of a duty or in the lawful exercise of a right or office; and 
•	That the injury caused or the offense committed be the necessary consequence of the due performance of duty or the lawful exercise of such right or office. 
The Supreme Court held that both requisites were met:
•	Lawful Duty: Cabanlig was an active police officer executing a direct, official mission to secure and escort a known, dangerous felon who was under lawful custody.
•	Necessary Consequence: A peace officer is duty-bound to prevent the escape of a high-risk detainee and prevent him from wreaking havoc or killing his captors. In an emergency inside a moving or confined vehicle where a desperate felon has initiated an aggressive struggle for an automatic assault rifle, the officer must make an instantaneous, split-second decision. Cabanlig fired to neutralize the imminent mortal peril facing the entire escort team and to prevent the felon's violent escape.
2. Yes. The requisites of self-defense and defense of a stranger/companion also concurred.
•	Unlawful Aggression: Jimmy Padilla’s violent assault on SPO2 Pader to forcibly seize a loaded high-powered firearm constituted real, immediate, and mortal unlawful aggression against the officers.
•	Reasonable Necessity of the Means Employed: Reversing the Sandiganbayan’s unrealistic standard that the officer should have aimed at non-vital body parts, the Court reiterated the settled rule in People v. Delima and related jurisprudence: a police officer locked in close-quarters mortal danger cannot be expected to exercise calm deliberation and aim with surgical precision. The test of reasonable necessity is one of rational equivalence, assessed from the standpoint of the defender under mortal peril, not retrospective mathematical or anatomical precision.
•	Lack of Sufficient Provocation: The police officers gave no unlawful provocation; they were conducting legitimate custodial duties.

### Ratio 

Dispositive:
The petition was granted. The Decision of the Sandiganbayan was reversed and set aside. Petitioner SPO2 Ruperto Cabanlig was acquitted of the crime of Homicide on the ground that his acts were fully justified under Article 11, paragraphs 1, 3, and 5 of the Revised Penal Code.
