### Art. 11. Justifying Circumstances. - The following do not incur any criminal liability:
* 1. Anyone who acts in defense of his person or rights, provided that the following circumstances concur: *
      First. Unlawful Aggression;
      Second. Reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repeal it; 
      Third. Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person defending himself.

This otherwise known as self-defense. Self-defense does not only include defense on one's life and limb. Self-defense also includes defense of one's honor and chastity, property if coupled by attack on the person entrusted with the said property. 

### Elements of Art. 11(1) - Self-Defense
1. Unlawful aggression;
2. Reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel it; and 
3. Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person defending himself.

### First Element - Unlawful Aggression
The SC said, the primordial element of self-defense is unlawful aggression. The unlawful aggression coming from the said offended party is material because without the unlawful aggression coming from the victim, there is no need for the accused to defend his life and limb. Hence, first and foremost, there must be an unlawful aggression coming from the victim. 

* Elements of Unlawful Aggression. * The unlawful aggression has three elements:
1. There must be a physical or material attack or assault; 
2. The attack or assault must be actual or at least imminent; and 
3. The attack or assault must be unlawful.

NOTE: 
An attack or assault is said to be actual when the attack or assault is done by means physical force or by means of a weapon.  
The attack or assault is said to be imminent when it is impending or at the point of happening.  
Again, unlawful aggression must come from the victim. It must come from the private offended party.

### Second Element - Reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel it
The means used to defend his life must be reasonable and rational in order to prevent unlawful aggression coming from the victim.
In order to determine whether the means used by the accused to defend his life is reasonable and rational, you take into consideration certain factors:
    1. Nature and number of weapons used by the said unlawful aggressor versus that of the person defending himself;
    2. Personal circumstances of the unlawful aggressor versus that of the person defending himself; and
    3. Place and occasion of the said assault.
These factors will determine if the means used by the accused in defending himself is reasonable and rational in order to prevent the unlawful aggression coming from the victim.

### Third Element - Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person defending himself
There must be no sufficient provocation. There may be provocation. What the law requires is that there is no sufficient provocation. Provocation is said to be sufficient when it is adequate for a person to commit a wrongful act and when it is proportionate to the gravity of the act.  
NOTE: The maxim behind self-defense is "Stand ground when in the right". The law does not require the accused to retreat when he sees his assailant fast approaching, otherwise, he runs the risk of being stabbed at the back.

### QUESTION: 
X was walking after withdrawing his salary from the ATM. He happily went towards home then suddenly three (3) men A, B and C, surrounded him. These men all pointed an icepick on X, telling him, "We saw you getting money from the ATM, give us your bag". X held tightly unto the bag and ran away. The three (3) men chased X and caught up with him, they then ganged up on X. All of them boxed and kicked him until X was down on the ground. When X was down on the ground, A forcible took his bag. A then went on top of X and was about to stab him with the icepick, but X was able to grab a huge piece of stone and hit A's head two (2) times. A bled and lost consciousness, the other men left. Seeing that he wounded A, X brought him to the hospital. It was the severe damage on the head that caused the death of the holdupper A.  
X is not being prosecuted for the death of the holdupper A. X raised self-defense. Are all the elements of self-defense present?
* ANSWER *
### YES. 
* First element: * There was unlawful aggression. A, B, and C surrounded X and ganged up on X. A was also about to stab X with an icepick. These acts placed X's life and limb in actual and imminent danger. Therefore, there is unlawful aggression on the part of the victim - the holdupper A.
* Second Element: * The means employed by X was reasonable and rational to repel the unlawful aggression. X was unarmed, A was about to stab him with an icepick, the only thing he could do to protect his life and limb was to use the stone he grabbed. It was rational, it was reasonable to hit the holdupper, otherwise, he could have been stabbed and could have died. Therefore, the second element is also present.
* Third Element: * Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person defending himself. There was no provocation at all coming from X, he was merely on his way home.  
All the elements of self-defense being present, X has to be acquitted of the crime charged by reason of self- defense.

### GANAL JR. v. PEOPLE G.R. NO. 248130 | DECEMBER 2, 2020 | LAZARO-JAVIER, J.
#### FACTS: 
When Follante went to the house of Ganal, Jr., he brought with him stones around two (2) inches in diameter for driving away dogs along the way. Ganal, Jr. saw the stones and ordered Follante to surrender them to him. Thereafter, he showed his gun to Follante and told the latter to go home if he did not want any trouble. Follante then proceeded to Alvarez' house and narrated the incident. Later on, Alvarez and Ganal, Jr. had a confrontation and in the course thereof, the latter shot Alvarez on the chest resulting to his death. After the incident, Ganal, Jr. voluntarily surrendered to the police authorities. He was charged with the crime of Homicide. In his defense, Ganal, Jr. claimed that Follante, armed with a knife and holding two (2) stones, advanced towards him and ignored his warning shot.  
#### ISSUE: 
W/N Ganal, Jr. acted in self-defense (YES)  
#### RULING: 
YES. All the elements of self-defense are present:
* Unlawful Aggression *
Here, stones were hurled at the roof of the adjacent houses of Ganal, Jr. and his father. The father went out to check and saw Follante with his uncle, Alvarez. The father approached and asked them to go home because his wife was suffering from hypertension and should not be disturbed. However, Alvarez replied that he did not care if the wife died - that he would kill all of them. Thereafter, Alvarez managed to push open the gate and hit Ganal, Sr. with a stone on the chest. Armed with a knife tucked in his waistband and holding two (2) stones, Alvarez turned to Ganal, Jr. and advanced towards him. Despite the warning shot of Ganal, Jr., Alvarez continued moving closer to Ganal, Jr. who was then constrained to shoot him.
*  Reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel unlawful aggression *
The right of a person to take life in self-defense arises from his belief in the necessity for doing so; and his belief and the reasonableness thereof are to be judged in the light of the circumstances as they then appeared to him, not in the light of circumstances as they would appear to others or based on the belief that others may or might entertain as to the nature and imminence of the danger and the necessity to kill.  
Here, Ganal, Jr. was overcome by the instinct of self- preservation upon seeing that Alvarez brashly entered his property and even knocked his father unconscious for getting in the way. Alvarez was determined to inflict injury on him as he brought two (2) large stones and knife for the purpose.  
Ganal, Jr. first tried to simply scare off Alvarez by firing a warning shot. However, Alvarez was unfazed and still continued to advance towards him with malevolent intent. And even after Ganal, Jr. shot him, the latter did not even falter but instead threatened to kill Ganal, Jr. and his family. In that particular instance, Ganal, Jr. must have thought that his actions were so futile because Alvarez was still standing there and shouting threats. He then must have felt he had to end it once and for all - kill or be killed.
* Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person making the defense *
It is undisputed that it was Alvarez who went to Ganal, Jr.'s house and instigated the incident. 

### PEOPLE v. OLARBE G.R. NO. 227421 | JULY 23, 2018 | BERSAMIN, J.
#### FACTS: 
Olarbe and his wife were suddenly awakened by the sound of a gunshot and shouting from Arca who appeared to be drunk. Armed with a rifle, Arca entered the house and aimed the gun at them. Olarbe grappled for the possession of the gun and shot Arca on the head. Nevertheless, Arca managed to get his bolo from his waist and continued to attack them. After struggling for its possession, Olarbe was able to grab the bolo and he instantly hacked Arca causing his death. Thereafter, Olarbe voluntarily surrendered to the police authorities. He was charged with the crime of Murder. In his defense, he interposed self-defense and defense of stranger.
#### ISSUE: 
W/N self-defense and defense of stranger should be appreciated (YES)
#### RULING: 
YES. 
For a person to exonerate himself on the ground of self-defense under the RPC, he must establish the following facts: (i) unlawful aggression on the part of the victim; (ii) reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel such aggression; and (iii) lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person resorting to self-defense. Olarbe also invoked the justifying circumstance of defense of a stranger. In both of these circumstances, the indispensable requisite for either is unlawful aggression mounted by the victim against the accused or the stranger. Without such unlawful aggression, the accused is not entitled to the justifying circumstance.
First, there was no credible showing that the shot to the head had rendered Arca too weak to draw the bolo and to carry on with his aggression in the manner described by Olarbe. Second, the State did not demonstrate that the shot from the gun fired at close range sufficed to disable Arca from further attacking with his bolo. Third, nothing in the record indicated Arca's physical condition at the time of the incident. And finally, to rule out any further aggression by Arca with his bolo after the shot in the head considering the fact that Arca would have enough adrenaline to continue with the assault is also speculative. The SC found that Arca committed continuous and persistent unlawful aggression against Olarbe and his common-law spouse that lasted from the moment he forcibly barged into the house and brandished his gun until he assaulted Olarbe's common-law spouse with a bolo. Since the assault was not merely a threatening fact, Olarbe was justified in believing that he and his common-law spouse's lives to be in extreme danger from Arca.
Going to the other requisites of self-defense, Olarbe was able to prove both. Reasonable necessity of the means employed is still present even if Arca sustained several wounds; however, these wounds were only lacerations whose nature and extent were not explained. The lack of explanations has denied the Court the means to fairly adjudge the reasonableness of the means adopted by Olarbe to repel the unlawful aggression. To rule out reasonable necessity on the basis of the number of wounds would be unfair to Olarbe since the rule of reasonable necessity is not ironclad in its application but is dependent upon the established circumstances of each particular case. The absence of any showing that Olarbe had provoked Arca, or that he had been induced by revenge, resentment, or other evil motive had been equally proven by the defense.

### NOTE:
In the cases of Ganal, Jr. v. People and People v. Olarbe, the SC appreciated self-defense in favor of the accused. In these cases, the SC said that the test to determine the presence of self-defense is whether the accused subjectively believed the imminence and seriousness of the danger, and the reasonableness of his belief must be viewed from the standpoint of the accused at the time that he acted. The right of the person to take life in self-defense arises from his belief in the necessity of doing so. That the reasonableness thereof must be judged in the light of the circumstances of the accused as they appear to him, not in the light of the circumstances as they appear to other people. Therefore, if at that particular moment, the accused believed his life was in danger, he has to save his life. So, in both cases the SC acquitted the accused on the ground of self-defense.

### ABUYO v. PEOPLE G.R. NO. 250495 | JULY 6, 2022 | M.V. LOPEZ, J. 
#### FACTS: 
At around 7:30 in the evening, Leo Abuyo and his wife were heading home on board their motorcycle. Cesar and Charles, armed with a balisong and a gun, suddenly blocked their way. Leo sped towards the father's house, Leonardo. Thereafter, Charles entered Leonardo's house. He pointed his gun to people and yelled for Leo to come out. Leonardo tried to pacify Charles, but Cesar arrived and stabbed Leonardo on the lower part of his chest. Cesar then turned to Leo and tried to stab him, but Leo got hold of a bolo on top of the table and hacked Cesar's right hand. Consequently, Cesar dropped the balisong. Cesar managed to pick up the balisong but Leo stabbed him again in the lower part of his stomach. Cesar died. After the incident, Leo voluntarily surrendered to the police authorities. He was charged for the crime of Homicide.
#### ISSUE: 
W/N Leo employed means that was reasonably necessary to repel the unlawful aggression. (YES)
#### RULING: 
YES. The elements of self-defense are: 
1. Unlawful aggression on the part of the victim; 
2. Reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel such aggression; and 
3. Lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person resorting to self-defense.

In defense of a relative, in lieu of the third element, the accused must prove that in case of provocation was given by the person attacked, that the one making the defense had no part therein.

Here, there was unlawful aggression when Cesar attacked Leonardo and attempted to stab Leo. Also, there was no provocation on Leo's part since the attack originated from Cesar and Charles. Leo and his wife were traversing their way home when Cesar and Charles blocked their way and chased them. Similarly, Leo was already in his father's house when Cesar and Charles started the commotion.

The rule is that the reasonable necessity of the means employed to repel or prevent the attack depends upon the imminent danger of injury. Cesar's act of attacking Leo and Leonardo with a balisong was a very real danger to their lives. Charles' possession of a gun exacerbated the danger that lurks on Leo and Leonardo's mortality. Leo had to repel the best way he can especially that Leonardo, who was already injured, could not be expected to aid in his defense. Lastly, that the stomach wound which Leo inflicted upon Cesar proved to be fatal did not make the means he employed any less reasonable under the circumstances.

### NOTE:
Here, Leo and his wife were going home on board a motorcycle. Suddenly, two (2) men namely, Ceasar, armed with a balisong and his son, Charles, armed with a gun blocked their path. Leo sensed danger and so he swerved his motorcycle to the left. Thereafter, Leo alighted from his motorcycle and ran towards the house of his father. Ceasar and Charles, however, did not stop. Charles followed Leo, he destroyed the bamboo fence, pointed a gun at the people around and shouted for Leo to come out of the house. Instead of Leo coming out it was Leo's father, Leonardo, who came out. Leonardo tried to pacify Charles, however, during this time, Ceasar arrived and stabbed Leonardo in the chest. Although he was wounded, Leonardo was able to run to the house, but at that particular moment, Ceasar still followed. Sensing that his father was about to be killed, Leo came out of the house and chased Ceasar. Ceasar then tried to stab Leo with his balisong, but Leo saw a bolo on top of the table, took it and hacked Ceasar. Ceasar died.
