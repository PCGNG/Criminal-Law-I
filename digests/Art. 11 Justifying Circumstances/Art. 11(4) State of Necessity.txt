4. Any person who, in order to avoid an evil or injury, does not act which causes damage to another, provided that the following requisites are present:  
First. That the evil sought to be avoided actually exists;  
Second. That the injury feared be greater than that done to avoid it;

### Elements of Art. 11(4) - State of Necessity
1. That the evil sought to be avoided actually exists;
2. That the injury feared be greater than that done to avoid it; and
3. That there be no other practical or less harmful means of preventing it.
It is necessary that the said accused is not the author of the state of necessity. If the accused authored the state of necessity, then he has no right to raise it as a defense.

### PEOPLE v. HERNANDEZ G.R. NO. 22553-R | APRIL 14, 1959 | GUTIERREZ DAVID, J.
FACTS: 
Hernandez and Lascano planned on getting married. They made the necessary wedding preparations, such as applied for a marriage license, went to the parish priest, ordered a wedding gown, etc. However, on the date of the wedding, Hernandez did not show up which caused great shame and humiliation to Lascano and his parents. Hernandez was then charged with the crime of Serious Slander by Deed. In her defense, she insisted that her non-appearance in the wedding is justified because, otherwise, she will be trapped in a loveless wedding which is the injury she is seeking to avoid.  
ISSUE: 
W/N Hernandez should be convicted of Serious Slander By Deed (NO)  
RULING: 
NO. The malice, one of the essential requisites of slander, has not been proven; that in the act done by Hernandez, there was no malice because in changing her mind, assuming that she was in love with Lascano previous to the incident, she was merely exercising her right not to give her consent to the marriage after mature consideration, such consent being her prerogative as one of the contracting parties.  The SC appreciated the justifying circumstance of the state of necessity. It agreed that the act of Hernandez in not appearing in the wedding is justified. Otherwise, it will be tantamount to mandating her to enter into a marriage [over] which she, in fact, has the choice of contracting into or not. Moreover, to rule otherwise would result in an injury that Norma seeks to avoid, that is, being trapped in a loveless wedding.

### NOTE: 
In the case of People v. Hernandez, Norma Hernandez has this wedding with the private complainant. It is a wedding based on the agreement of the elderlies, and Norma Hernandez didn't love the man. And so, she did not appear on the date of the wedding because of the embarrassment, because of the pain. Because of the acts done by Norma Hernandez, it brought embarrassment on the part of the said private complainant, the groom-to-be, as well as the family, and so, Norma was charged with Slander by Deed. To free herself both from criminal and civil liability, she raised as a defense the justifying circumstance of state of necessity. Should she be absolved of criminal liability? Should she be absolved of civil liability? Is there state of necessity as a justifying circumstance?
*First element:* That the evil sought to be avoided actually exists. SC said that it is present. The SC said that the evil sought to be avoided by Norma was to live in a loveless marriage with this man all her life.
*Second element:* That the injury feared be greater than that done to avoid it. The injury that she feared to be with this man whom she doesn't love all her life is greater than the embarrassment of not appearing in the said wedding.  
*Third element:* That there be no other practical and less harmful means of preventing it. There is no other way she could prevent herself from being in that situation except not to appear in the said wedding.  
The SC said all the elements of state of necessity is present. Therefore, Norma Hernandez is acquitted for the crime of Slander by Deed. She acted in a state of necessity, otherwise, she will be living a life of a loveless marriage with this man whom she does not love.  
In state of necessity, it does not mean that the offender is also not civilly liable. Although as a rule, in case of justifying circumstances, an offender is not criminally and civilly liable. An exception to that rule is in case of paragraph four - state of necessity. In case of state of necessity, the offender is not criminally liable, but civilly liable.  
In this case, although Norma was acquitted of criminal liability for Slander by Deed, she was held liable civilly. So, yes, state of necessity is present. However, the SC said that she is liable civilly. There were already expenses because of the wedding preparations.
