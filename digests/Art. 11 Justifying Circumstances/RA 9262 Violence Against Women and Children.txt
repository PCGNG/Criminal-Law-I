SECTION 26. Battered Woman Syndrome as a Defense. - Victim-survivors who are found by the courts to be suffering from battered woman syndrome do not incur any criminal and civil liability notwithstanding the absence of any of the elements for justifying circumstances of self-defense under the RPC.  
In the determination of the state of mind of the woman who was suffering from battered woman syndrome at the time of the commission of the crime, the courts shall be assisted  by  expert  psychiatrists/psychologists.

### QUESTION
X and Y are married. They had been married for five (5) years. During those five (5) years of marriage, the wife has suffered gravely at the hands of her husband. During those five (5) years of marriage, the wife has been the victim of physical abuse from the said husband. Every night, whenever the husband would arrive home, the husband would physically abuse the wife, slap the wife, kick the wife; and the following morning, when the husband would see the hematoma, the bruises, the contusions, on the face, on the different parts of the body of the wife, the husband would ask the wife for forgiveness, the husband would be loving to the wife. But, come nighttime, whenever he's drunk, again, he would physically abuse the said wife. This has been going on every day of the life of the wife for five (5) years.  
One night, the husband arrived home, again he is drunk. Upon seeing the husband, the wife told the husband, "Kumain ka muna bago ka humiga. Pinaghanda kita ng hapunan." The husband said, "Walang hapu-hapunan sakin. Bakit mo ba ako pinagha-handaan ng hapunan?" And thereafter, the husband immediately punched the wife two (2) times on the face. The wife fell on the floor. She went off balance. She shouted back at the husband, "Bakit naman?" When the wife answered back, the husband again got mad and kicked the wife. The husband pulled out a knife and was about to stab the wife, but the wife shouted. Because of this, the husband placed the knife and the cupboard and went to bed and fell asleep.
The wife went to the bedroom to get clothing for their children. As she was getting out of the bedroom, she passed by the bed where her husband was fast asleep. As she was staring at the husband, she was shivering, she was so afraid. All five (5) years of her life, she was afraid of this man. In fact, she was already under treatment and medication by psychiatrists. As she was staring at this man, tonight this man was about to stab her. She was afraid, tomorrow she might die. The next day, her children might be killed. According to the wife, everything must end. So, she went to the kitchen, took a knife, and thereafter, the wife stabbed the husband while the husband was fast asleep.
The wife is now being prosecuted for the crime of Parricide. The wife raises as a defense of Battered Woman Syndrome, therefore, was moving for an acquittal.
### NOTE: 
Battered Woman Syndrome, under Sec. 3(C) of RA 9262, is the scientifically defined pattern of psychological symptoms found in women living in battering relationship as a result of cumulative abuse. 
Under Sec. 26 of RA 9262, victim-survivors who are found by the court suffering from Battered Woman Syndrome do not incur any criminal liability nor civil liability, notwithstanding, the absence of any of the elements of the justifying circumstance of self-defense.  
Therefore, based on Secs. 3(C) and 26 of RA 9262, if it is proven in court that the said accused woman, accused wife, is suffering from Battered Woman Syndrome, she incurs no criminal liability and no civil liability, even if none of the elements of the justifying circumstance of self- defense is present, even if there was no unlawful aggression coming from the victim, even if the husband was fast asleep when he was stabbed to death by the wife.
A: In this case, when the said woman raised the defense of Battered Woman Syndrome, the counsel of the woman placed on the witness stand the psychiatrist who has been treating the wife for five (5) years. The psychiatrist testified in open court that this woman has been her patient for five (5) years, and that she is indeed suffering from Battered Woman Syndrome at the time of the stabling of the said husband.  
What is now the duty of the court? The court has to acquit the said woman, no civil liability and no criminal liability, even if none of the elements of the justifying circumstance of self-defense is present, even if there was no unlawful aggression coming from the victim, even if the husband was fast asleep at the time of the said act of stabbing, at the time of the said killing. 
Under the second paragraph of Sec. 26, in order to help the court to determine the state of mind of the said woman at the time of the commission of the crime, the law mandates that there must be an expert testimony coming from psychiatrists and psychologists because judges, by themselves, cannot determine whether the said woman is suffering from Battered Woman Syndrome. Only the psychiatrists and psychologists can testify to the fact that this woman, at the time of the commission of the crime, was in the state of Battered Woman Syndrome. If proven, she shall be acquitted of both criminal and civil liability.

### PEOPLE v. GENOSA G.R. NO. 135981 | JANUARY 15, 2004 | PANGANIBAN, J.
FACTS: 
Marivic and Ben were married. However, after a year of marriage, the spouses would often quarrel which results to violence. In those instances, Ben, who was a habitual drinker, would provoke, slap, beat, or pin Marivic down on the bed. She had tried to leave her husband at least five (5) times, but Ben would always follow her and ask for forgiveness. Marivic was then eight months pregnant. One night, there was a quarrel between Marivic and Ben. Frightened that her husband would hurt her and wanting to make sure she would deliver her baby safely, Marivic shot her Ben while he was sleeping. She was then charged with the crime of Parricide. Upon her interview, Marivic was shown to have been suffering from Battered Woman Syndrome.
ISSUE: 
W/N Battered Woman Syndrome should be appreciated (NO)
RULING: 
NO. The battered woman syndrome is characterized by the so-called "cycle of violence, which has three phases: (i) the tension-building phase; (ii) the acute battering incident; and (iii) the tranquil, loving (or, at least, nonviolent) phase.  
In this case, the defense fell short of proving all three phases of the "cycle of violence" supposedly characterizing the relationship of Ben and Marivic. No doubt that there were acute battering incidents. In relating to the Court how the fatal incident that led to the death of Ben started, Marivic perfectly described the tension-building phase of the cycle. She was able to explain in adequate detail the typical characteristics of this stage. However, that single incident does not prove the existence of the syndrome. In other words, she failed to prove that in at least another battering episode in the past, she had gone through a similar pattern.
How did the tension between the partners usually arise or build up prior to acute battering? How did Marivic normally respond to Ben's relatively minor abuses? What means did she employ to try to prevent the situation from developing into the next (more violent) stage? 
Neither did Marivic proffer sufficient evidence in regard to the third phase of the cycle. She simply mentioned that she would usually run away to her mother's or father's house; that Ben would seek her out, ask for her forgiveness and promise to change; and that believing his words, she would return to their common abode.  
In sum, the defense failed to elicit from Marivic her factual experiences and thoughts that would clearly and fully demonstrate the essential characteristics of the syndrome.

### BAR QUESTION
During their marriage, the husband has always been verbally abusing the wife. The wife will just be crying in the corner because of the pain, the emotional pain brought about by the verbal abuse. One time, however, the husband arrived home, this time it was not only verbal abuse, the husband also physically abused the said wife for the first time. That night, the said wife killed the husband. The wife is now being prosecuted for the crime of Parricide. Can she raise the defense of Battered Woman Syndrome?
*ANSWER*
A: NO. She cannot raise the defense of Battered Woman Syndrome. Battered Woman Syndrome is the scientifically defined pattern of psychological symptoms found in women living in battering relationships. Therefore, it is necessary that the woman is living in a battering relationship. 
What is battery? If you will look at Sec. 3(B) of RA 9262, "battery" refers to an act of inflicting physical harm upon the woman or her child resulting to the physical and psychological or emotional distress. Therefore, in order to be said that the woman was living in a "battering relationship," physical harm must be inflicted upon the said woman, and not merely verbal abuse. 
In the En banc case of People v. Genosa, the SC said that there must be two battering episodes of abuses, and these two battering episodes of abuses must consist of a cycle of violence characterized by three phases.
These  three phases of characterizing  battering relationships are:  
1. Tension building phase, 
2. Acute battering phase, and 
3. Tranquil or loving phase.  
In the Bar problem given, there was only one physical abuse. All other instances were verbal abuse. Therefore, it cannot be said that there were two battering episodes. The said woman cannot raise as a defense of Battered Woman Syndrome.
