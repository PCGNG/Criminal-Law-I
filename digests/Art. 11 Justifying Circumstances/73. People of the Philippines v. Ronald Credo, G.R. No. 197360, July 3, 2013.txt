### Fact
On the evening of June 22, 2005, in Pili, Camarines Sur, the victim, Joseph Nicolas, was playing bingo at a local bingohan. Appellant Randy Credo arrived, approached Joseph, and abruptly punched him in the chest, causing Joseph to fall down. Randy immediately fled toward his house. 

When word reached Ronald Credo (Randy’s brother), Ronald armed himself with a bolo and set out to find Joseph. Their father, Rolando Credo, followed. Meanwhile, Joseph’s children went looking for their father and encountered the appellants, who threw stones at them. Shortly thereafter, prosecution eyewitnesses—including a neighbor looking through his window jalousies—saw Ronald, Randy, and Rolando corner Joseph and repeatedly hack him with bolos. Another brother, Roger Credo, was heard shouting, "Tama na Manoy, gadan na!" ("Enough brother, he is already dead!"). The autopsy revealed six severe hack wounds on Joseph's body, the fatal one cutting into the left lateral neck area. 

Ronald Credo invoked defense of a relative under Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code. He claimed that while walking home, Joseph suddenly emerged from behind their house brandishing a bolo and was about to attack Ronald's mother, compelling Ronald to hack Joseph to protect her. Rolando and Randy interposed alibi and denial, claiming they did not participate in the hacking. 
The Regional Trial Court (RTC) convicted Ronald, Randy, and Rolando of Murder qualified by abuse of superior strength and sentenced them to reclusion perpetua. The Court of Appeals (CA) affirmed the conviction. During the pendency of the appeal before the Supreme Court, Rolando died, extinguishing his criminal liability. 

On 22 June 2005, at about 10:30 p.m. in Barangay San Jose, Pili, Camarines Sur, Joseph Nicolas was attacked and fatally hacked. An amended Information charged the three appellants with murder alleging abuse of superior strength as a qualifying circumstance. The prosecution presented eyewitnesses — Joseph’s son Russel and nephew Francis — who testified that they saw the three appellants, each armed with a bolo, repeatedly hack the victim. Manuel Chica corroborated seeing the three appellants armed together. The medico-legal report showed six hack wounds, one of which was fatal.

### Issue 
Whether Ronald Credo proved by clear and convincing evidence that he acted in legitimate defense of a relative under Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code.

### Ruling 
1. No. Ronald failed to prove the indispensable requisite of unlawful aggression.
Just like self-defense, defense of a relative requires unlawful aggression as an indispensable condition sine qua non. Without unlawful aggression on the part of the victim, defense of a relative cannot be appreciated, complete or incomplete. 
•	There was no credible proof that Joseph initiated an attack or brandished a bolo against Ronald's mother. The defense’s narrative was inconsistent and uncorroborated, whereas the prosecution eyewitnesses positively established that the appellants hunted down the victim and ambushed him.
•	Even assuming an earlier altercation occurred at the bingohan, any aggression by Joseph had completely ceased. When unlawful aggression ceases, the person who continues the assault is acting out of retaliation or revenge, not defense.
