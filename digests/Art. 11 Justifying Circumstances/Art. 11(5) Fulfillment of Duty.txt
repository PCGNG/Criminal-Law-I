ARTICLE 11. Justifying circumstances. - The following do not incur any criminal liability:  
xxxx
5. Any person who acts in the fulfillment of a duty or in the lawful exercise of a right or office.
xxxx
*Elements of Art. 11(5) - Fulfillment of Duty*  
1. That the offender acted in the due performance of his duties or in the performance of a lawful exercise of a right or office; and 
2. That the resulting felony is an unavoidable or necessary consequence of the due performance of his duty or in lawful exercise of a right or office.  
In order for fulfillment of duty to lie, it is not only necessary that the said offender is performing his duty; it is also necessary that whatever injury that was caused by him, it was the unavoidable consequence of the due performance of his duty.

### PEOPLE v. DAGANI G.R. NO. 153875 | AUGUST 16, 2006 | AUSTRIAMARTINEZ, J.
FACTS: 
Javier and his group were having a drinking spree at the canteen located inside the compound of the Philippine National Railways (PNR). Dagani and his co-accused suddenly entered the canteen and approached the group of Javier. Dagani then shoved Miran, causing the latter to fall from his chair. Thereafter, he held Javier while his co-accused Santiano shot Javier twice at his left side resulting to his death. Dagani and his co-accused were charged with the crime of Murder. In their defense, they interposed self-defense and lawful performance of official duty as PNR security officers.  
ISSUE: 
W/N self-defense should be appreciated (NO)  
RULING: 
NO. Self-defense requires that there be (i) an unlawful aggression by the person injured or killed by the offender, (ii) reasonable necessity of the means employed to prevent or repel that unlawful aggression, and (iii) lack of sufficient provocation on the part of the person defending himself. All these conditions must concur.  Unlawful aggression, a primordial element of self- defense, would presuppose an actual, sudden and unexpected attack or imminent danger on the life and limb of a person - not a mere threatening or intimidating attitude - but most importantly, at the time the defensive action was taken against the aggressor. To invoke self-defense successfully, there must have been an unlawful and unprovoked attack that endangered the life of the accused, who was then forced to inflict severe wounds upon the assailant by employing reasonable means to resist the attack.
The defense was unable to prove that there was unlawful aggression on the part of Javier. They were unable to present evidence that the victim actually fired his gun. No spent shells from the .22 caliber pistol were found and no bullets were recovered from the scene of the incident. Javier also tested negative for gunpowder residue.
Even if it were established that Javier fired his gun as the appellants so insist, the imminence of the danger to their lives had already ceased the moment Dagani held down the victim and grappled for the gun with the latter. After the victim had been thrown off-balance, there was no longer any unlawful aggression that would have necessitated the act of killing.  
It is settled that reasonable necessity of the means employed does not imply material commensurability between the means of attack and defense. What the law requires is rational equivalence.  
The circumstances in their entirety which surround the grappling of the firearm by Dagani and Javier, such as the nature and number of gunshot wounds sustained by the victim which amounted to two fatal wounds, that Dagani was able to restrain the hands of Javier and push them away from his body, that Dagani was larger than Javier and had finished Special Weapons and Tactics (SWAT) hand-to-hand combat training, and Javier, as admitted by the appellants, was inebriated at the time of the incident, do not justify appellant Santiano's act of fatally shooting the victim twice.

### NOTE: 
In the case of People v. Dagani, the security guards at the PNR shot the victim. They raised the defense of fulfillment of duty. The SC said that the first element is immediately absent. It cannot be said that the said security guards were acting in the due performance of their duty because based on the evidence, based on the timecard presented, they were already off duty at that time. Their duty has already lapsed at the time of the incident. Since they were off duty, the first element is immediately absent. Hence, this fulfillment of duty as a justifying circumstance was not considered by the SC.
