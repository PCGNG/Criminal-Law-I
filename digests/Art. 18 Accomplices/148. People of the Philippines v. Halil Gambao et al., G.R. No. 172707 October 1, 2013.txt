### **Case Brief: People of the Philippines v. Halil Gambao, et al.**

*G.R. No. 172707, October 1, 2013*

---

### **Facts**

One evening, Theng Dilangalen (Dilangalen) and an armed companion forcibly dragged Lucia Chan (Chan) from her residence in Pasay City into a van and transported her to a safehouse, where she was detained and guarded by Halil Gambao (Gambao), Eddie Karim (Karim), Edwin Dukilman (Dukilman), Tony Abao (Abao), Raul Udal (Udal), Teng Mandao (Mandao), Jaman Macalinbol (Macalinbol), Monette Ronas (Ronas), and Nora Evad (Evad) while the abductors demanded a ₱400,000.00 ransom from Chan's son.

During Chan's detention, Thian Perpenian *a.k.a.* Larina Perpenian (Perpenian)—then 17 years old—entered the room where Chan was kept and conversed with Evad and Ronas regarding stories unrelated to the kidnapping. Later, police operatives intercepted the van carrying the ₱400,000.00 ransom, arrested Gambao, Karim, Dukilman, and Abao, and rescued Chan from a resort cottage in Calamba, Laguna, where Dilangalen, Udal, Macalinbol, Mandao, Ronas, Evad, and Perpenian were apprehended.

The RTC convicted all eleven accused as principals in Kidnapping for Ransom, which the CA affirmed with modification (reducing Perpenian's penalty to *reclusion perpetua* on account of her minority). On automatic review before the SC, Perpenian questioned the sufficiency of the evidence establishing her liability as a co-principal in the kidnapping.

---

### **Issue**

Is Thian Perpenian criminally liable as a co-principal in conspiracy, or merely as an accomplice under the Revised Penal Code, for the crime of Kidnapping for Ransom?

---

### **Ruling**

**Accomplice only.** The Supreme Court ruled verbatim:

> "Seeing that the only evidence the prosecution had was the testimony of Chan to the effect that on 13 August 1998 Perpenian entered the room where the victim was detained and conversed with Evad and Ronas regarding stories unrelated to the kidnapping, this Court opines that Perpenian should not be held liable as a co-principal, but rather only as an accomplice to the crime."

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, Revised Penal Code** (Principals)
* **Article 18, Revised Penal Code** (Accomplices)
* **Article 267, Revised Penal Code, as amended by R.A. No. 7659** (Kidnapping and serious illegal detention)
* **Republic Act No. 9344** (Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006 — minority and discernment)
* **Republic Act No. 9346** (Prohibiting the imposition of the death penalty)
* *People v. De Vera*, 371 Phil. 563 (1999) (Elements of accomplice liability)
* *People v. Clemente, et al.*, 128 Phil. 268 (1967) (Resolution of doubt in favor of the milder form of criminal liability in kidnapping for ransom)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

To hold an accused liable as a co-principal in a conspiracy under Articles 8 and 17 of the Revised Penal Code, the prosecution must establish beyond reasonable doubt that the accused participated in the criminal resolution or performed an act indispensable to the commission of the crime. Where the sole proven act of an accused is entering the room where a kidnap victim is detained and conversing with the abductors—showing knowledge of the unlawful detention and lending simultaneous moral support and company, without participating in the abduction, ransom demand, or any act essential to the perpetration of the kidnapping—any doubt as to the degree of her participation must be resolved in favor of the milder form of responsibility, rendering her liable merely as an accomplice under Article 18 of the Revised Penal Code.

---

### **Fallo**

The Supreme Court **AFFIRMED WITH MODIFICATIONS** the June 28, 2005 Decision of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CR-H.C. No. 00863:

1. Found accused-appellants **Halil Gambao, Eddie Karim, Edwin Dukilman, Tony Abao, Raul Udal, Theng Dilangalen, Jaman Macalinbol, Monette Ronas, and Nora Evad** guilty beyond reasonable doubt as **principals** in the crime of Kidnapping for Ransom and sentenced them to suffer the penalty of ***reclusion perpetua*, without eligibility for parole**;
2. Found accused-appellant **Thian Perpenian y Rafon *a.k.a.* Larina Perpenian** guilty beyond reasonable doubt as an **accomplice** in the crime of Kidnapping for Ransom, and—taking into account her minority at the time of the offense and having acted with discernment—sentenced her to suffer the indeterminate penalty of **six (6) months and one (1) day of *prision correccional*, as minimum, to six (6) years and one (1) day of *prision mayor*, as maximum**, ordering her **immediate release** from the Correctional Institution for Women for having already fully served said penalty;
3. Dismissed the case as to **Teng Mandao** by reason of his supervening death prior to final judgment; and
4. Ordered the convicted accused-appellants to pay Lucia Chan **₱100,000.00 as civil indemnity, ₱100,000.00 as moral damages, and ₱100,000.00 as exemplary damages** (apportioned at **₱288,000.00** among the nine principals and **₱12,000.00** to accomplice Perpenian, subject to Article 110 of the Revised Penal Code), with **6% interest per annum** from finality until fully paid.

---

### **Elaborate Answer to Review Question**

**1. What is the reason for the Supreme Court’s ruling that Thian Perpenian is not a principal but only an accomplice?**

The Supreme Court downgraded Thian Perpenian's liability from a **co-principal** (under **Articles 8 and 17**) to an **accomplice** (under **Article 18 of the Revised Penal Code**) because the prosecution's evidence satisfied the three elements of accomplice liability while falling short of proving conspiracy or indispensable cooperation:

1. **Absence of Proof of Conspiracy or Indispensable Participation (Articles 8 and 17, RPC):**
Unlike her co-accused—who forcibly abducted Lucia Chan, guarded her at gunpoint, transported her between safehouses, and negotiated or collected the ₱400,000.00 ransom—Perpenian was not shown to have planned the kidnapping, abducted the victim, demanded ransom, or stood guard to prevent Chan's escape. As the Supreme Court explicitly noted, **Perpenian's presence and company were not indispensable and essential to the perpetration of the kidnapping for ransom**; the principals could have fully consummated the detention and extortion without her.
2. **Satisfaction of the Three Elements of Accomplice Liability (*People v. De Vera*):**
Citing *People v. De Vera*, the Court applied the three requisites required to hold a person liable as an **accomplice** under **Article 18 of the Revised Penal Code**:
* **First — Community of Design (Knowledge and Concurrence):** Perpenian entered the room where Chan was being held captive and casually conversed with abductors Evad and Ronas, and was later apprehended alongside the kidnappers during the police rescue at Elizabeth Resort. A rational person encountering a detained victim under such circumstances would have realized an unlawful detention was occurring and reported it to the police; instead, Perpenian chose to remain with the captors (and later lied under oath about her identity and presence out of fear of being linked to people she knew had committed a wrong), demonstrating her **knowledge of and concurrence in** the principals' criminal design.
* **Second — Cooperation by Previous or Simultaneous Acts (Moral Aid):** By keeping Evad and Ronas company inside the room where Chan was detained, Perpenian lent **simultaneous moral aid and support** to the abductors during the continuing detention of the victim.
* **Third — Relation Between the Acts of the Principals and the Accomplice:** Her simultaneous presence and companionship were directly connected to and supportive of the principals' ongoing act of illegally detaining Chan.


3. **Resolution of Doubt in Favor of the Milder Form of Responsibility (*People v. Clemente, et al.*):**
Guided by *People v. Clemente, et al.*, the Supreme Court emphasized that in kidnapping for ransom, where the evidence leaves reasonable doubt as to whether an accused acted as a co-conspirator/principal or merely lent secondary, non-essential assistance, **the doubt must be resolved in favor of the milder form of criminal liability**—that of an **accomplice** rather than a principal.
