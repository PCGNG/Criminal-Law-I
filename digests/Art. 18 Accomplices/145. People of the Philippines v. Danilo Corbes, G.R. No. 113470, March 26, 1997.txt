### **Case Brief: People of the Philippines v. Danilo Corbes y Olazo and Manuel Vergel y Pascual**

*G.R. No. 113470, March 26, 1997*

---

### **Facts**

One morning, six armed men barged into the premises of Caloocan Consortium Corporation in Caloocan City, divested the establishment and an employee of cash, and fatally shot security guard Timoteo Palicpic before taking his .38 caliber revolver. Meanwhile, Danilo Corbes (Corbes)—who had looked for a getaway vehicle and convinced Manuel Vergel (Vergel) to drive—waited with Vergel aboard a parked blue passenger jeep along 8th Avenue.

Moments after the robbery, the armed men ran toward 8th Avenue and hurriedly boarded the parked jeep as Vergel urged them, *"Dalian n'yo, baka tayo mahuli!"* ("Hurry up, we might get caught!"), before Vergel drove the group away from the crime scene. The RTC convicted Corbes and Vergel as principals by conspiracy for the special complex crime of Robbery with Homicide under Article 294(1) of the Revised Penal Code, prompting their appeal to the SC.

---

### **Issue**

Are Danilo Corbes and Manuel Vergel criminally liable as principals by conspiracy for Robbery with Homicide, or merely as accomplices to the crime of robbery under the Revised Penal Code?

---

### **Ruling**

**Accomplices to the crime of robbery only.** The Supreme Court ruled that in the absence of clear and convincing proof of conspiracy—and where the accused's participation was limited to procuring and driving the getaway vehicle without having joined in the decision to commit the robbery or having any knowledge of or opportunity to prevent the killing—any doubt as to the degree of criminal participation must be resolved in favor of the milder form of liability, rendering them liable merely as accomplices to robbery under Article 18 in relation to Articles 294(5) and 295 of the Revised Penal Code.

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, Revised Penal Code** (Principals)
* **Article 18, Revised Penal Code** (Accomplices)
* **Articles 294(5) and 295, Revised Penal Code** (Robbery committed by a band)
* *People v. Doble*, 114 SCRA 131 (1982)
* *People v. Tamayo*, 61 Phil. 225 (1935)
* *People v. Balili*, 17 SCRA 892 (1966)
* *People v. Adriano*, 95 SCRA 107 (1980)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

Conspiracy must be established by the same quantum of proof required to establish the crime itself—proof beyond reasonable doubt—and cannot rest on mere surmises or conjectures. When an accused did not participate in the planning or decision to commit a robbery, and his cooperation is limited to providing and driving a getaway vehicle with knowledge of the criminal design—an act that merely facilitates the escape rather than being indispensable to the execution of the robbery—he is not a co-conspirator or principal under Article 17, but a mere accomplice under Article 18 of the Revised Penal Code. Furthermore, an accomplice in robbery cannot be held liable for a homicide committed by the principals inside the establishment where there is no proof of an agreement to kill and his position outside in the getaway vehicle gave him no opportunity to prevent the killing.

---

### **Fallo**

> "WHEREFORE, the judgment appealed from is MODIFIED. Accused-appellants DANILO CORBES Y OLAZO and MANUEL VERGEL y PASCUAL are declared GUILTY merely as ACCOMPLICES to the crime of robbery and sentenced accordingly to an indeterminate prison term of four (4) months of *arresto mayor* medium as minimum to four (4) years and two (2) months of *prision correccional* medium as maximum, and to indemnify Caloocan Consortium Corporation in the amount of ₱169,000.00 and its employee in the amount of ₱4,500.00, plus ₱5,000.00 for the stolen .38 caliber revolver, without subsidiary imprisonment in case of insolvency.
> Accused-appellants Danilo Corbes y Olazo and Manuel Vergel y Pascual having already been detained for more than the maximum of their indeterminate penalty, their immediate release from custody is likewise ordered unless they are held for another lawful cause. Costs *de oficio*.
> SO ORDERED."

### **Elaborate Answer to Review Question**

**What are the legal and factual basis of the Supreme Court’s ruling that Manuel Vergel is not liable as a principal but only as an accomplice?**

The Supreme Court downgraded Manuel Vergel's criminal liability from a **principal by conspiracy** (under **Articles 8 and 17**) to a **mere accomplice** (under **Article 18 of the Revised Penal Code**) based on clear doctrinal distinctions in criminal law and the specific facts surrounding his participation:

* **A. Legal Basis:**
1. **Distinction Between Conspirators (Principals) and Accomplices (Articles 8, 17, and 18, RPC):**
* **Conspirators / Principals (Articles 8 and 17):** Conspirators *decide* that a crime should be committed; they are the authors of the felony who conceive and resolve upon the criminal design, or who cooperate by an act **without which the crime would not have been accomplished** (principal by indispensable cooperation).
* **Accomplices (Article 18):** Accomplices are persons who, not being included in Article 17, cooperate in the execution of the offense by **previous or simultaneous acts**. Accomplices do *not* decide whether the crime should be committed; they come to know about the criminal design *after* the principals have already reached the decision, and merely assent to the plan and concur in its execution by supplying material or moral aid in an efficacious—yet **dispensable**—way.


2. **Rule in Case of Doubt as to Degree of Participation (*People v. Doble* and *People v. Tamayo*):**
Settled jurisprudence holds that conspiracy must be proved as clearly and convincingly as the commission of the crime itself. Where the evidence fails to establish beyond reasonable doubt that the driver of a getaway vehicle participated in the original criminal resolution or that his cooperation was indispensable to the robbery, **any doubt as to whether the accused acted as a principal or as an accomplice must be resolved in favor of the milder form of criminal liability** (that of an accomplice).
3. **Non-Liability of an Accomplice in Robbery for an Unplanned Homicide:**
While all *principals* in a robbery are generally liable for a homicide committed on the occasion thereof under Article 294(1), the same rule does not automatically extend to a mere *accomplice* in the robbery when the killing was not part of the criminal plan and the accomplice had no opportunity to prevent it.


* **B. Factual Basis:**
1. **Recruited Only After the Criminal Plan Was Already Formed:** Vergel was not present when the six armed men and mastermind ("Benny") hatched the plan to rob Caloocan Consortium Corporation. He was merely approached afterward by Corbes, who had been tasked to look for a vehicle, and convinced to drive the passenger jeep. Thus, Vergel did not *decide* on the robbery; he merely *concurred* in a pre-existing criminal design.
2. **Knowledge and Cooperation Proved by His Conduct at the Getaway Jeep:** Although Vergel claimed ignorance of the robbery, his act of waiting at the parked jeep along 8th Avenue and urgently telling the fleeing robbers, *"Dalian n'yo, baka tayo mahuli!"* ("Hurry up, we might get caught!"), conclusively proved that he knew of their unlawful purpose and intentionally supplied material aid to facilitate their escape.
3. **Indispensability Test — Driving the Jeep Was Not Essential to the Robbery:** The six armed men entered the consortium on foot and consummated the taking of cash entirely on their own. Vergel's act of driving the getaway jeep merely facilitated their flight; the robbery itself could still have been accomplished without his vehicle.
4. **No Agreement to Kill and No Opportunity to Prevent the Homicide:** The evidence showed that the robbers had no agreement to kill—in fact, one of the robbers even berated the gunman for shooting the security guard. Because Vergel remained stationed outside in the parked jeep along 8th Avenue, he neither knew of the shooting nor had any chance to prevent it. Consequently, he was held liable only as an **accomplice to robbery in band**.
