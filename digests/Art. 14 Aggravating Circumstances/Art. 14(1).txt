### ART. 14(1). That advantage be taken by the offender of his public position. 
The offender obviously is a public officer, because only a public officer can take advantage of his public position in committing the crime. A public officer is said to have taken advantage of his public position in the commission of the crime when the said public officer used. misused, or abused his public position in order to commit the crime. The said public officer took advantage of the prestige, the ascendancy, and the influence that his office affords him in order to commit the crime.
### QUESTION 1:
A public officer was charged with the crime of Estafa by taking advantage of his public position. The public officer  committed  Estafa  through misappropriation and conversion under Art. 315, 1(b) by taking advantage of his public position in order to ensure the commission of the crime. It was proven that he was taking advantage of his public position. If you were the judge, how will you appreciate taking advantage of one's public position in committing the crime of Estafa?  
### **ANSWER**
It would be considered as INHERENT or absorbed because the crime committed is Other Frauds under Art. 214 of the RPC. Under Art. 214, it is committed by a public officer who by taking advantage of his public position commits any of the act of Estafa under Arts. 315, 316, 317, or 318. In this case, taking advantage of his public position is simply absorbed as it is an inherent element in the commission of the crime. It is an inherent aggravating circumstance.

### QUESTION 2:
A public officer took advantage of his public position in falsifying a public document. How shall the court appreciate taking advantage of one's public position?
### **ANSWER**
It would be considered as an INHERENT aggravating circumstance. Under Art. 171 of the RPC, in case of falsification of a public document, it is an element that the public officer must have taken advantage of his public position.

### QUESTION 3:
A police officer arrested a woman, placed the woman behind bars, took the woman, and raped her inside the precinct. How shall the court appreciate taking advantage of one's public position?
### **ANSWER**
The crime committed is Qualified Rape. Taking advantage of his public position to commit the crime of rape is a QUALIFYING aggravating circumstance. It changes the nature of the rape from Simple Rape punished by reclusion perpetua to Qualified Rape punished by death penalty.

### PEOPLE v. URAL G.R. NO. L-30801 | MARCH 27, 1974 | AQUINO, J.
### **FACTS:**
Alberto, a former detention prisoner, decided to sleep in the municipal building. Upon arrival, he witnessed Policeman Ural inside the jail cell boxing the Napola. Napola collapsed on the floor. Thereafter, Ural stepped on Napola's prostrate body. Ural went out of the cell and eventually returned with a bottle. He poured its contents on Napola's recumbent body. Thereafter, he ignited it with a match and left the cell. Napola screamed in agony. He shouted for help, but nobody came to help him. When Alberto left the municipal building, Ural warned him not to tell what he saw. Later on, Napola died. Ural was charged with the crime of Murder.  
### **ISSUE:**
W/N taking advantage of public position was present (YES)  
### **RULING:** 
YES. Under Art. 14, paragraph 1, a public officer is said to have taken advantage of his public position in the commission of the crime when he used, misused, or abused his public position in the commission of the crime.  In the case at bar, the trial court correctly held that the accused took advantage of his public position (Par. 1, Art. 14, RPC). He could not have maltreated Napola if he was not a policeman on guard duty. Because of his position, he had access to the cell where Napola was confined. The prisoner was under his custody.

### QUESTION 4:
A police officer extorted money by taking advantage of his public position. Charged with robbery, it was alleged in the Information that he took advantage of his public position in order to commit the act of Robbery. How shall the court appreciate taking advantage of one's public position?
### **ANSWER**
It shall be considered as a SPECIAL aggravating circumstance. In the old case of People v. Ural, the SC appreciated taking advantage of one's public position. However, the SC considered it at that time as a generic aggravating circumstance and therefore, offset it. Since it was offset, it was no longer present. However, now it can no longer be offset because based on Art. 62, as amended by RA 7659, a public officer taking advantage of his public position in the commission of the crime, it will be considered as a special aggravating circumstance because the law provides that the maximum penalty prescribed by law shall be the one imposed. It is a special aggravating circumstance which cannot be offset by any ordinary mitigating circumstance.
