### **Case Brief: Gloria Macapagal Arroyo v. People**

*G.R. No. 220598, July 19, 2016*

---

### **Facts**

The Ombudsman charged former President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo (Arroyo), PCSO General Manager Rosario Uriarte (Uriarte), PCSO Budget and Accounts Manager Benigno Aguas (Aguas), and several other PCSO officials with Plunder before the Sandiganbayan. The Information alleged that the accused conspired to amass, accumulate, and acquire ₱365,997,915.00 in ill-gotten wealth by diverting PCSO Confidential/Intelligence Funds (CIF) to their personal use through repeated cash advances, without identifying a specific "main plunderer."

During trial, the prosecution presented evidence showing that Uriarte requested additional CIF releases supported by brief justifications, which Arroyo approved by writing unqualified marginal notes ("OK") on the requests. Meanwhile, Aguas certified the availability of funds, signed disbursement vouchers, and issued certifications that the cash advances were liquidated without submitting the supporting receipts to the Commission on Audit (COA), invoking confidentiality rules.

After the prosecution rested its case, Arroyo and Aguas filed demurrers to evidence, arguing that the prosecution failed to establish the elements of plunder and failed to prove any conspiracy among them. The Sandiganbayan denied their demurrers, prompting Arroyo and Aguas to file petitions for *certiorari* before the SC.

---

### **Issue**

Are Gloria Macapagal Arroyo and Benigno Aguas criminally liable as co-principals by conspiracy for the crime of plunder based on Arroyo's unqualified "OK" marginal notations and Aguas's certifications in the disbursement and liquidation of PCSO Confidential/Intelligence Funds?

---

### **Ruling**

**No.** The Supreme Court ruled verbatim:

> "To establish the common design or purpose, the Prosecution must prove that each of the accused committed overt acts that were linked to each other in such a way as to show their unity of purpose in committing the crime of plunder. In this case, the Prosecution failed to prove that any implied conspiracy existed among Arroyo, Aguas, and Uriarte."

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, Revised Penal Code** (Principals)
* **Sections 1(d) and 2, Republic Act No. 7080** (Anti-Plunder Law)
* **Letter of Instruction (LOI) No. 1282** (Guidelines on the release and liquidation of Confidential and Intelligence Funds)
* **COA Circular No. 92-385** and **COA Circular No. 2003-002** (Audit and liquidation procedures for intelligence/confidential funds)
* *Estrada v. Sandiganbayan*, G.R. No. 148560, November 19, 2001 (Elements of Plunder and the "main plunderer" requirement)
* *Arias v. Sandiganbayan*, G.R. No. 81563, December 19, 1989 (Doctrine on reliance of heads of offices on subordinates)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

To hold accused persons liable as co-principals in a wheel conspiracy for plunder, the prosecution must both allege and prove the identity of the "main plunderer" (the hub) who amassed, accumulated, or acquired ill-gotten wealth of at least ₱50,000,000.00, and demonstrate through overt acts that the alleged co-conspirators (the spokes) shared a common criminal design to enrich that main plunderer. Mere performance of official, ministerial, or regular administrative duties—such as a President affixing an unqualified "OK" approval on CIF requests or an accounting officer certifying the availability and liquidation of funds in accordance with existing regulations—does not establish an implied conspiracy in the absence of proof of irregular participation, personal benefit, or fraudulent intent.

---

### **Fallo**

> "WHEREFORE, the Court GRANTS the petitions for *certiorari*; ANNULS and SETS ASIDE the resolutions issued in Criminal Case No. SB-12-CRM-0174 by the Sandiganbayan on April 6, 2015 and September 10, 2015; GRANTS the petitioners' respective demurrers to evidence; DISMISSES Criminal Case No. SB-12-CRM-0174 as to the petitioners GLORIA MACAPAGAL-ARROYO and BENIGNO AGUAS for insufficiency of evidence; ORDERS the immediate release from detention of said petitioners; and MAKES no pronouncements on costs of suit.
> SO ORDERED."

---

### **Elaborate Answer to Review Question**

**1. What are the legal and factual basis of the decision that the Prosecution failed to allege properly the elements of the crime, and to prove that any implied conspiracy to commit plunder or any other crime existed among Gloria Macapagal Arroyo, Aguas, and Uriarte?**

The Supreme Court's ruling that the prosecution failed both in its pleading and in its proof rests on distinct legal and factual grounds under Republic Act No. 7080 and Articles 8 and 17 of the Revised Penal Code:

* **A. Legal Basis — Failure to Properly Allege the Elements of Plunder (The "Main Plunderer" Requirement):**
1. **Statutory Requirement of a "Main Plunderer":** Under Section 2 of R.A. No. 7080, plunder is committed by a public officer who, by himself or in connivance with others, amasses, accumulates, or acquires ill-gotten wealth through a combination or series of overt criminal acts in the aggregate amount of at least ₱50,000,000.00. In a wheel conspiracy for plunder (as established in *Estrada v. Sandiganbayan*), there must be a central figure—the **"main plunderer"** (the hub)—to whom the ill-gotten wealth accrues, while the co-conspirators serve as the spokes.
2. **Defective Information:** The Information filed by the Ombudsman lumped all ten accused together without identifying who among them was the main plunderer or who actually amassed, accumulated, or acquired the ₱365,997,915.00. Because the Information failed to specify the main plunderer, the Sandiganbayan could not legally determine who bore primary liability for the accumulation of ill-gotten wealth and who acted merely as cohorts, violating the accused's constitutional right to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation against them.


* **B. Legal and Factual Basis — Failure to Prove an Implied Conspiracy Among Arroyo, Aguas, and Uriarte:**
Under Article 8 of the Revised Penal Code, conspiracy is never presumed; it must be proven with the same quantum of evidence required to establish the crime itself—proof beyond reasonable doubt. Where no express agreement is shown, an **implied conspiracy** requires proof of coordinated, irregular overt acts pointing to a single unlawful objective. The Court found that the individual acts of Arroyo and Aguas were lawful official acts, not overt acts of a criminal conspiracy:
1. **As to Gloria Macapagal Arroyo:**
* *Unqualified "OK" Marginal Notations:* Arroyo's sole proven participation was writing "OK" on Uriarte's letter-requests for additional CIF. Under LOI No. 1282, the President is authorized to approve releases of intelligence and confidential funds. Writing "OK" was a standard, regular exercise of presidential approval, not an irregular act.
* *Absence of Fraudulent Intent or Personal Benefit:* The prosecution presented zero evidence that Arroyo received a single centavo from the released funds, that she knew Uriarte would misappropriate the money, or that she instructed Uriarte to divert the funds. Under the *Arias* doctrine, a head of office has the right to rely to a reasonable extent on the representations and regularity of actions of subordinates; approving a request on its face does not make the approving authority a co-conspirator in a subordinate's subsequent malversation or plunder.


2. **As to Benigno Aguas:**
* *Regular Performance of Accounting Duties:* Aguas's actions—certifying the availability of funds, signing disbursement vouchers, and certifying that the cash advances were liquidated—were ministerial functions required of him as PCSO Budget and Accounts Manager.
* *Compliance with COA Rules:* Under COA Circulars No. 92-385 and No. 2003-002, supporting documents for confidential and intelligence expenses are not submitted to the resident auditor, but are sealed and transmitted directly to the COA Chairman. Thus, Aguas's issuance of liquidation certifications without attaching detailed receipts to the PCSO accounting records conformed to existing audit regulations rather than proving a cover-up.
* *No Proof of Connivance or Enrichment:* Like Arroyo, there was no evidence showing that Aguas profited from the CIF releases or acted in concert with Uriarte to siphon public funds.




* **C. Failure to Prove "Raid on the Public Treasury" (Section 1[d][6], R.A. No. 7080):**
Finally, the prosecution failed to prove the predicate act of a "raid on the public treasury." Under the statutory construction doctrine of *noscitur a sociis*, a "raid on the public treasury" must be of the same nature as misappropriation, conversion, misuse, or malversation—requiring proof that the public funds were unlawfully taken for the **personal benefit** of the accused. Because the prosecution failed to trace the ₱365,997,915.00 to the personal pockets of Arroyo, Aguas, or even Uriarte, neither plunder nor any lesser included offense (such as malversation) was established beyond reasonable doubt.
