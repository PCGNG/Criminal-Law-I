### **Case Brief: Jose "Jinggoy" Estrada v. Sandiganbayan**

*G.R. No. 148965, February 26, 2002*

---

### **Facts**

Following impeachment proceedings against former President Joseph Ejercito Estrada, the Office of the Ombudsman filed an Amended Information for Plunder under Republic Act No. 7080 before the Sandiganbayan against Joseph Estrada, his son Jose "Jinggoy" Estrada (then Mayor of San Juan), and several other co-accused.

The Amended Information alleged that Joseph Estrada, in connivance and conspiracy with his co-accused, amassed ill-gotten wealth through four distinct predicate acts detailed in sub-paragraphs (a) to (d): (a) receiving protection money from illegal gambling (*jueteng*), in which Jinggoy Estrada was specifically named as a co-conspirator; (b) misappropriating tobacco excise tax shares; (c) ordering the GSIS and SSS to purchase shares of Belle Corporation and collecting commissions; and (d) unjustly enriching himself from kickbacks and gifts.

Jinggoy Estrada moved to quash the Amended Information, arguing before the SC that he could not be charged with plunder alongside the other alleged conspirators because he was only named in sub-paragraph (a) (*jueteng* payoffs) and had no connection whatsoever with the conspirators in sub-paragraphs (b) to (d), contrary to the principle that criminal liability is personal and not vicarious.

---

### **Issue**

May Jose "Jinggoy" Estrada be validly indicted as a co-principal in a single Information for plunder alongside co-accused who participated in separate predicate acts, without violating the principle that criminal liability is personal rather than vicarious?

---

### **Ruling**

**Yes.** The Supreme Court ruled verbatim:

> "The case at bar is similar to the so-called 'wheel' conspiracy in American jurisdiction. The hub is former President Estrada while the spokes are all the accused, and the rim that encloses the spokes is the common goal in the overall conspiracy, i.e., the amassing, accumulation and acquisition of ill-gotten wealth."

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, Revised Penal Code** (Principals)
* **Sections 1(d) and 2, Republic Act No. 7080** (Anti-Plunder Law)
* **Section 6, Rule 110, Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure**
* *People v. Quitlong*, 354 Phil. 372 (1998)
* *Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750 (1946) & *United States v. Bruno*, 105 F.2d 921 (1939) (Doctrines on Wheel and Chain Conspiracies)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

Under Philippine criminal law, when conspiracy is alleged not as a separate substantive offense, but as a mode of committing a crime, the liability of the conspirators is collective, making each participant equally liable as a principal under the principle that the act of one is the act of all. In a "wheel conspiracy" where a central figure (the hub) deals individually with different groups or individuals (the spokes) who are all united by a single overarching criminal objective (the rim)—here, amassing ill-gotten wealth for the main plunderer—all conspirators may be validly joined in a single Information for plunder. However, each spoke is held criminally accountable only for the specific predicate acts committed in conspiracy with the hub, and cannot be held vicariously liable for separate conspiracies entered into exclusively between the hub and the other spokes.

---

### **Fallo**

> "IN VIEW WHEREOF, the petition is dismissed for failure to show that the respondent Sandiganbayan acted without or in excess of jurisdiction or with grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack of jurisdiction.
> SO ORDERED."

---

### **Elaborate Answers to Review Questions**

**1. How did the Supreme Court resolve the argument of Estrada that he should not be charged with the crime of plunder together with the alleged conspirators, because he is not even remotely connected with said conspirators contrary to the principle that criminal liability is personal, not vicarious?**

The Supreme Court rejected Jinggoy Estrada's argument to dismiss the charge, harmonizing his joinder in the single Information with the principle of personal criminal liability through two core legal points:

* **A. Unity of Purpose Under a Single Overarching Conspiracy (The "Rim"):**
In the crime of plunder, different individuals may perform distinct, seemingly unrelated illegal acts, yet remain united by a single common criminal design. As alleged in the Amended Information, the gravamen of the conspiracy charge was not that every accused personally agreed to collect *jueteng* protection money, divert tobacco excise taxes, and manipulate SSS/GSIS stock purchases simultaneously. Rather, the common purpose that bound all of them together was that each accused, through their respective individual roles, agreed to participate directly or indirectly in **amassing, accumulating, and acquiring ill-gotten wealth for former President Joseph Estrada**. Because R.A. No. 7080 was enacted specifically to capture systemic, large-scale looting of public funds in a single prosecution—avoiding the multiplicity of suits that plagued earlier ill-gotten wealth cases—joining all co-conspirators in one Information was procedurally and substantively proper.
* **B. Limitation of Vicarious Accountability to Specific Predicate Acts:**
To preserve the fundamental rule that criminal liability is personal and not vicarious, the Supreme Court clarified the exact scope of Jinggoy Estrada's criminal exposure. The Court observed that the Amended Information did not clearly allege that the accused in sub-paragraphs (a), (b), (c), and (d) conspired *with one another*; rather, it alleged that each group conspired *with former President Estrada*. Consequently, the Court held that **Jinggoy Estrada can only be held accountable for the predicate acts he allegedly committed in sub-paragraph (a) (*jueteng* payoffs) in conspiracy with former President Estrada**, and **cannot be penalized for the separate conspiracies entered into by the other accused in sub-paragraphs (b) to (d)**. Furthermore, because sub-paragraph (a) alleged that he received *jueteng* money *"on several instances,"* his own participation already satisfied the statutory requirement of a "series" of overt criminal acts under R.A. No. 7080.

---

**2. What kind of multiple conspiracies are present in this case? What is the basis?**

Drawing from established jurisprudence, the Supreme Court distinguished between two structures of multiple conspiracies—**wheel conspiracy** and **chain conspiracy**—and classified the plunder scheme in this case as a **Wheel Conspiracy**:

* **1. The Two Kinds of Multiple Conspiracies Recognized by the Court:**
* **Wheel Conspiracy:** Occurs when there is a single person or group (the **"hub"**) dealing individually with two or more other persons or groups (the **"spokes"**). The spokes typically interact directly with the hub rather than with each other. If the spokes share a common overarching objective to succeed (the **"rim"**), they form a single overarching conspiracy (or connected spokes within one wheel); if each spoke is wholly unconcerned with the success of the other spokes, they constitute separate, independent conspiracies (as illustrated in *Kotteakos v. United States*).
* **Chain Conspiracy:** Exists when there is successive communication and horizontal/vertical cooperation among participants operating in sequential stages—much like legitimate business operations moving from manufacturer to wholesaler, wholesaler to retailer, and retailer to consumer—to achieve a single criminal objective (as illustrated in *United States v. Bruno*).


* **2. Classification and Basis in *Estrada v. Sandiganbayan*:**
The Supreme Court held that the structure of the plunder charge in *Estrada* is a **Wheel Conspiracy**:
* **The Hub:** Former President **Joseph Ejercito Estrada**, who occupied the central position of power and dealt separately with different sets of associates and family members.
* **The Spokes:** All the **co-accused** grouped across sub-paragraphs (a) to (d) of the Amended Information (such as Jinggoy Estrada in the *jueteng* payoffs, and different sets of cohorts in the tobacco excise tax diversion, Belle Corporation share purchases, and kickbacks), each dealing directly with the hub.
* **The Rim:** The **common criminal goal** enclosing all the spokes—namely, the amassing, accumulation, and acquisition of at least ₱50 million in ill-gotten wealth of and/or for former President Joseph Estrada.
