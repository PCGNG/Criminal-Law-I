### **Case Brief: People of the Philippines v. Ramon Regalario, Marciano Regalario, Sotero Regalario, Bienvenido Regalario, and Noel Regalario**

*G.R. No. 174483, March 31, 2009*

---

### **Facts**

During a public dance and singing contest in Barangay Natasan, Libon, Albay, Rolando Sevilla (Sevilla) and his companion were approached by Barangay Kagawad Sotero Regalario (Sotero). Although Sevilla tried to distance himself to avoid trouble, a commotion broke out during which Sotero and Barangay Tanod Bienvenido Regalario (Bienvenido) repeatedly struck Sevilla with their wooden nightsticks (*bahi*).

Sevilla managed to get up and fled toward the house of Barangay Captain Marciano Regalario (Marciano), pursued by Sotero and Bienvenido. As Sevilla neared the residence, Barangay Tanod Ramon Regalario (Ramon) emerged from the side and waylaid him, while Marciano and his son, Noel Regalario (Noel)—who was carrying a seven-inch knife—came out of their house. The five men surrounded Sevilla in front of Marciano's house and took turns beating him with nightsticks until he collapsed face down on the ground. Marciano then boxed Sevilla on the jaw and shouted orders to kill him and tie him up. Complying with Marciano's directive, Bienvenido, assisted by Sotero, hog-tied the motionless victim around his neck, hands, and feet using a carabao nylon rope while Ramon, Marciano, and Noel stood watch. Sevilla died of severe blood loss resulting from stab wounds on his neck and multiple lacerated wounds on his head.

The RTC convicted all five accused of Murder, and the CA affirmed the conviction. On automatic review before the SC, the accused argued that the lower courts erred in finding that they conspired to kill Sevilla without direct proof of a prior agreement.

---

### **Issue**

Are Ramon, Marciano, Sotero, Bienvenido, and Noel Regalario criminally liable as co-principals in conspiracy for the murder of Rolando Sevilla despite the absence of direct proof of an explicit prior agreement to kill him?

---

### **Ruling**

**Yes.** The Supreme Court ruled that conspiracy exists when two or more persons come to an agreement concerning the commission of a felony and decide to commit it, and affirmed the findings of the lower courts that the five accused acted in conspiracy. Direct proof of a previous agreement is not essential to establish conspiracy; it may be inferred from the coordinated and synchronized acts of the accused before, during, and after the commission of the offense showing a joint purpose, concerted action, and community of interest.

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, Revised Penal Code** (Principals)
* **Article 248, Revised Penal Code, as amended by R.A. No. 7659** (Murder qualified by abuse of superior strength, attended by the generic aggravating circumstance of outraging or scoffing at the corpse)
* **Republic Act No. 9346** (Prohibiting the imposition of the death penalty)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

Under Article 8 in relation to Article 17 of the Revised Penal Code, direct evidence that the perpetrators met beforehand and expressly agreed to commit a felony is not necessary to prove conspiracy. Conspiracy is sufficiently established when the concerted overt acts of the accused—from initiating the assault, pursuing and blocking the victim's escape, taking turns in beating the helpless victim with weapons, to obeying the order to hog-tie him—demonstrate a unity of criminal design and common objective. Once conspiracy is proven, all participants become co-principals, and the act of one becomes the act of all, rendering each accused equally liable for the resulting murder regardless of who inflicted the fatal stab or blunt-force wounds.

---

### **Fallo**

The Supreme Court **AFFIRMED with MODIFICATIONS** the May 31, 2006 Decision of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CR No. 01556:

1. Reduced the penalty of death imposed on accused-appellants Ramon, Marciano, Sotero, Bienvenido, and Noel Regalario to ***reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole** pursuant to Republic Act No. 9346;
2. Ordered the accused-appellants to pay the heirs of Rolando Sevilla, jointly and severally, **₱75,000.00 as civil indemnity**, **₱75,000.00 as moral damages**, and **₱30,000.00 as exemplary damages**; and
3. Imposed **legal interest at the rate of 6% per annum** on all monetary awards from the date of finality of the decision until fully paid.

---

### **Elaborate Answers to Review Questions**

**1. How may the existence of conspiracy be established?**

Under **Article 8 of the Revised Penal Code**, conspiracy exists when two or more persons come to an agreement concerning the commission of a felony and decide to commit it. Because criminal conspiracies are rarely hatched in the open or documented through explicit agreements, settled jurisprudence recognizes two ways by which conspiracy may be established:

* **A. By Direct Evidence:** Through direct testimony or proof showing that the perpetrators actually met beforehand and expressly agreed in words or writing to enter into and pursue a common criminal design.
* **B. By Circumstantial Evidence (Implied Conspiracy):** Direct proof of an explicit prior agreement is not indispensable. Conspiracy may be deduced from the **mode, method, and manner** in which the offense was perpetrated, or inferred from the **coordinated, synchronized acts of the accused before, during, and after the commission of the crime**. When the acts of the several accused—taken together—point to a **joint purpose, concerted action, and community of interest**, the assent of their minds is legally inferred as parts of a single complete whole. Once established, every conspirator becomes a **co-principal** under Article 17 of the Revised Penal Code under the doctrine that **the act of one is the act of all**, regardless of the specific degree or character of each individual's physical participation.

---

**2. What factual circumstances did the Supreme Court consider as proof of a conspiracy between the accused?**

The Supreme Court held that the prosecution's eyewitness testimonies overwhelmingly established a chain of synchronized, mutually reinforcing acts proving that Ramon, Marciano, Sotero, Bienvenido, and Noel Regalario acted with a single criminal objective to kill Rolando Sevilla:

1. **Initiation of the Attack and Coordinated Pursuit:** Sotero and Bienvenido started the assault at the barangay pavilion by repeatedly striking Sevilla with their wooden nightsticks (*bahi*). When Sevilla managed to stand up and run away, Sotero and Bienvenido immediately pursued him to prevent his escape.
2. **Strategic Interception (Waylaying the Victim):** As Sevilla fled toward Marciano's residence, Ramon emerged from the left side of the road and deliberately waylaid Sevilla, blocking his path of escape and trapping him in front of Marciano's house.
3. **Simultaneous Reinforcement with Weapons:** At that exact moment, Marciano and his son Noel emerged from their house—with Noel armed with a seven-inch knife, while Ramon, Sotero, and Bienvenido were armed with wooden nightsticks (*bahi*).
4. **Collective, Alternating Physical Assault:** Having surrounded Sevilla in front of Marciano's house, the accused took turns beating the victim with their weapons until he slumped face down on the ground. Even after Sevilla had collapsed, Marciano punched him on the jaw.
5. **Issuance and Execution of Commands to Kill and Hog-Tie the Victim:** While Sevilla lay motionless on the ground, Marciano—the Barangay Captain and patriarch—shouted orders to kill him and tie him up. In immediate obedience to Marciano's command, Bienvenido fetched a carabao nylon rope and, with Sotero's assistance, hog-tied Sevilla's neck, hands, and feet while Ramon, Marciano, and Noel stood beside them watching.
6. **Post-Crime Cover-Up:** Following the killing, Marciano and Sotero went to the police station ahead of the victim's widow and entered a fabricated blotter report claiming that Sevilla had shot Ramon and was still alive under the custody of barangay tanods, further demonstrating their unified effort to conceal their crime.
