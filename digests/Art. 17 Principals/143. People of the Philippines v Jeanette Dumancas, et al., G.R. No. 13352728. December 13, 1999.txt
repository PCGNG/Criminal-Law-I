### **Case Brief: People of the Philippines v. Jeanette (Ginette) Yanson-Dumancas, et al.**

*G.R. Nos. 133527-28, December 13, 1999*

---

### **Facts**

Jeanette Yanson-Dumancas (Dumancas) was swindled of ₱352,000.00 in a fake gold bar transaction by Danilo Lumangyao (Lumangyao) and Rufino Gargar, Jr. (Gargar). In retaliation, a group of police officers and civilian agents led by Dominador Geroche (Geroche) and Mario Lamis (Lamis), acting under the direction of Police Col. Nicolas Torres (Torres), plotted and executed the abduction of Lumangyao and Gargar, detaining them across several motels.

While the victims were already detained at D'Hacienda Motel, Dumancas arrived to question them regarding the whereabouts of her swindled money. When Lumangyao and Gargar replied that the money had already been spent, Dumancas allegedly told Geroche to "take care of the two," while also instructing that they be brought to the police so proper cases could be filed. Meanwhile, Police Inspector Adonis Abeto (Abeto) merely interrogated the captives and implemented a search warrant at a suspect's residence under superior orders. Later, Geroche and his group blindfolded, handcuffed, and fatally shot Lumangyao and Gargar, and had their bodies buried in a shallow grave.

The RTC convicted Dumancas as a principal by inducement and Abeto as a co-conspirator for Kidnapping for Ransom with Murder, prompting their appeal to the SC.

---

### **Issue**

Are Jeanette Yanson-Dumancas and Adonis Abeto criminally liable as principals by inducement or co-conspirators under the Revised Penal Code for the kidnapping and murder of Danilo Lumangyao and Rufino Gargar, Jr.?

---

### **Ruling**

**No.** The Supreme Court ruled that the prosecution failed to establish beyond reasonable doubt that Dumancas acted as a principal by inducement or that Abeto participated in the criminal conspiracy, holding that a remark to "take care of the two" uttered after the abduction had already occurred—especially when accompanied by instructions to turn the victims over to the police—does not constitute the direct, determining command required under Article 17(2) of the Revised Penal Code, and that mere presence or performance of official investigative duties under superior orders does not establish conspiracy.

**Legal Basis / Doctrines Relied Upon:**

* **Article 8, Revised Penal Code** (Conspiracy and proposal to commit felony)
* **Article 17, paragraph 2, Revised Penal Code** (Principals by inducement / directly forcing or inducing others to commit a felony)
* *United States v. Indanan*, 24 Phil. 203 (Requisites of principal by inducement: intent to procure the crime and inducement as the determining cause)
* *People v. Castillo*, 17 SCRA 721 (Inducement must precede the commission of the offense)
* *People v. Manambit*, 271 SCRA 344 (Equipoise rule: when evidence is susceptible of two interpretations, one consistent with innocence and one with guilt, the accused must be acquitted)

---

### **Ratio Decidendi**

To hold an accused liable as a principal by inducement under Article 17(2) of the Revised Penal Code, the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that the inducement was made prior to the commission of the crime with the direct intention of procuring it, and that such inducement was the determining cause of the felony through a prize, reward, promise, or unmistakable words of command. Ambiguous remarks spoken after the abduction was already consummated—and contradicted by testimony showing the accused intended the captives to be turned over to police authorities for legal prosecution—fail to meet this standard. Likewise, conspiracy cannot be inferred from mere presence, companionship, or the routine performance of official investigative duties under superior orders absent proof of a shared criminal design to abduct and kill.

---

### **Fallo**

The Supreme Court **ACQUITTED** accused-appellants Jeanette Yanson-Dumancas and Adonis Abeto for failure of the prosecution to prove their guilt beyond reasonable doubt and ordered their immediate release from detention; **DISMISSED** the case and appeal as to Police Col. Nicolas Torres by reason of his supervening death pending appeal; and **AFFIRMED** the convictions of the remaining accused-appellants (with a modification reducing the penalty and civil liability of accessory Cesar Pecha, and adjusting the joint and several civil indemnities of the convicted principals to ₱50,000.00 death indemnity, ₱50,000.00 moral damages, and ₱25,000.00 exemplary damages for each case).

---

### **Elaborate Answer to Review Question**

**Why were the accused Dumancas and Abeto acquitted as principals by inducement?**

The Supreme Court acquitted Jeanette Yanson-Dumancas and Adonis Abeto based on distinct evidentiary and doctrinal deficiencies under **Article 17(2)** (Principals by Inducement) and **Article 8** (Conspiracy) of the Revised Penal Code:

* **A. Grounds for the Acquittal of Jeanette Yanson-Dumancas:**
1. **Absence of the Two Modes of Direct Forcing or Inducing (Article 17[2], RPC):**
Under settled jurisprudence (*United States v. Indanan*), a person becomes a principal by inducement only through two recognized modes: *(a) by giving a price, offering a reward, or making a promise*, or *(b) by using words of command*. Furthermore, two indispensable requisites must concur: **(1)** the inducement must be made directly with the intention of procuring the commission of the crime, and **(2)** such inducement must be the **determining cause** of the commission of the crime by the material executors—requiring on the part of the inducer *"the most positive resolution and the most persistent effort to secure the commission of the crime, together with the presentation to the person induced of the very strongest kind of temptation"*. Here, Dumancas had no participation in the planning meeting where the abductors hatched the plot, nor was there proof that she used irresistible force, caused uncontrollable fear, or offered any reward or payment prior to the crime.
2. **Timing of the Alleged Remark (*People v. Castillo*):**
For words of command to constitute criminal inducement, the utterance must **precede** the commission of the felony. When Dumancas visited the motel and allegedly told Geroche to *"take care of the two,"* Lumangyao and Gargar had already been abducted and illegally detained by Geroche's group. Thus, her statement could not have induced an abduction that had already occurred.
3. **Ambiguity of the Remark and Application of the Equipoise Rule (*People v. Manambit*):**
Even assuming Dumancas uttered the words *"take care of the two,"* the phrase was equivocal and fell far short of an explicit directive to execute the victims. Crucially, prosecution witness Moises Grandeza himself admitted on cross-examination that Dumancas remarked that since the swindling happened months earlier, the money was likely gone, and instructed that the victims be brought to the police so proper charges could be filed against them. Under the **equipoise rule**, when inculpatory facts are susceptible of two interpretations—one consistent with innocence (turning the swindlers over to authorities) and the other with guilt—the constitutional presumption of innocence mandates an acquittal.


* **B. Grounds for the Acquittal of Police Inspector Adonis Abeto:**
1. **No Participation in the Criminal Conspiracy:**
Abeto was not charged or proven to have induced anyone, nor was he part of the secret planning or execution of the abduction and murder. Settled doctrine holds that mere presence at the scene or companionship with the perpetrators does not establish conspiracy without proof of a shared unlawful design.
2. **Performance of Regular Official Duties Under Superior Orders:**
Abeto's sole involvement consisted of questioning the detainees and serving/implementing a search warrant at the residence of one of the suspects pursuant to orders from his superior officer, Police Col. Torres. Because his actions were consistent with the lawful performance of police functions, the **constitutional presumption of innocence** and the **presumption of regularity in the performance of official duty** prevailed—placing him in the exact same position as co-accused Police Officers Canuday and Pahayupan, whom the trial court had earlier acquitted for insufficiency of evidence.
