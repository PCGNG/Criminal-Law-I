An accessory does not know the criminal design of the principal concurred in by an accomplice. What the accessory knows is that a crime has been committed, and despite having knowledge that a crime has been committed, still, he participated after the commission of the crime. He takes part subsequent to the commission of the crime. Hence, his participation is after the fact, after the commission of the crime.

### P.D. 1612 ANTI-FENCING LAW
#### ACCESSORY
1. The crime committed by the principal may be any crime. 
2. The prosecution has the burden of proving beyond reasonable doubt that the accused charged as an accessory has knowledge that a crime has been committed.

#### FENCE
1. The crime committed by the principal must only be Robbery or Theft. Fencing will not apply to any other crime.
2. possession  the  for  a  prima  value  The law does not require that he has knowledge that a crime of Robbery or Theft has been committed because it suffices that it should have been known to him that a thing in his is proceeds of Robbery or Theft.  
Reason: Under Sec. 5 of PD 1612, the law provides facie presumption of fencing.  
Under Sec. 5 of PD 1612, any person found in possession of any article, item, object, or anything of which proceeds of Robbery or Thievery, presumes that he is a fence.  is  the  the  law

Example: A valuable ballpen was lost. It was found in the possession of Mario. Mario can be held liable as a fence under PD 1612. He can be charged as a fence because the law presumes that he ought to know that the thing in his possession is the proceeds of Robbery or Thievery. The burden is on him to rebut the presumption provided for by law under Sec. 5 of PD 1612.

*Second Act* -– By concealing or destroying the body of the crime, or the effects or instruments thereof, in order to prevent its discovery.
Body of the crime/substance of the crime/corpus delicti refers to the fact that a crime has been committed by someone.

*Elements of Body of the Crime*
1. Proof of occurrence of certain event; and
2. Proof of a person's criminal responsibility therefor.

Example: X killed Y. After X killed Y, he looked around. He believes no one saw him killed Y. He cut the deceased body of Y into pieces, placed them inside a sack, placed the sack in the trunk of his car, and drove his car near the ocean. Thereafter, he threw the cut pieces of the body of Y into the ocean.
But unknown to X, all the criminal acts that he did were witnessed by W. W saw the killing. W saw X cutting the body of Y into pieces. W saw X placing the deceased body of Y inside a sack. W followed X as X went to the ocean and threw the cut pieces of the body of Y into the ocean.
Even if the prosecution could not find the deceased body of Y in the ocean, X can still be convicted. The body of the crime is the fact that W, as a witness, saw everything. W can prove the occurrence of the killing, and W can point to X as the one responsible for the killing of Y. Hence, there still exists the body of the crime/substance of the crime/corpus delicti by reason of the testimony to be provided by W, the witness.

*Third act* -- By harboring, concealing, or assisting in the escape of the principals of the crime, provided the accessory acts with abuse of his public functions or whenever the author of the crime is guilty of treason, parricide, murder, or an attempt to take the life of the Chief Executive, or is known to be habitually guilty of some other crime
** Two kinds of accessory under the third act**
1. Public officer - If the accessory who harbors, conceals, or assists in the escape of the principal is a public officer, the crime committed by the principal may be any crime, provided that the act of harboring, concealing, or assisting in the escape of the principal is done by the public officer by abusing his public functions.
2. Private individual - If, however, the act of harboring, concealing, or assisting in the escape of the principal is done by a private person, what the law requires is that the author of the crime is guilty of Treason. Parricide. Murder. or an Attempt to take the life of the Chief Executive, or is known to be habitually quilty of some other crime.

### PEOPLE v. DULAY G.R. NO. 193854 | SEPTEMBER 24, 2012 | PERALTA, J. 
*FACTS:*
AAA was 12 years old when the whole incident happened. AAA's sister introduced the Dulay to AAA as someone who is nice. Thereafter, Dulay convinced AAA to accompany her at a wake in Paranaque City. AAA, appellant and the latter's boyfriend proceeded to the Kubuhan located at the back of the Bulungan Fish Port. When they reached the Kubuhan, AAA suddenly pulled AAA inside a room where a man known by the name "Speed" was waiting. AAA saw "Speed" give money to appellant and heard "Speed" tell appellant to look for a younger girl. Thereafter, "Speed" wielded a knife and tied AAA's hands to the papag and raped her. AAA asked for AAA's help when she saw the latter peeping into the room while she was being raped, but appellant did not do so. After the rape, "Speed" and appellant told AAA not to tell anyone what had happened or else they would get back at her. Dulay was charged with the crime of Rape under Article 266- A(1)(a) of the RPC in relation to Section 5(b) of RA 7610.

* ISSUE: *
W/N Dulay is criminally liably for the crime of Rape as co-principal by indispensable cooperation. (NO)

*RULING:* 
NO. An accused may be considered a principal by direct participation, by inducement, or by indispensable cooperation. To be a principal by indispensable cooperation, one must participate in the criminal resolution, a conspiracy or unity in criminal purpose and cooperation in the commission of the offense by performing another act without which it would not have been accomplished.
Nothing in the evidence presented by the prosecution does it show that the acts committed by appellant are indispensable in the commission of the crime of rape. The events narrated by the CA, from the time appellant convinced AAA to go with her until appellant received money from the man who allegedly raped AAA, are not indispensable in the crime of rape. Anyone could have accompanied AAA and offered the latter's services in exchange for money and AAA could still have been raped. Even AAA could have offered her own services in exchange for monetary consideration and still end up being raped. Thus, this disproves the indispensable aspect of the appellant in the crime of rape. It must be remembered that in the Information, as well as in the testimony of AAA, she was delivered and offered for a fee by appellant, thereafter, she was raped by "Speed."
In the case of People v. Dulay, Dulay was charged as a principal by indispensable cooperation in the crime of Rape. The prosecution alleged that without Dulay's act, Speed would not be able to commit the act of rape on the girl.  
When the case reached the SC, the Court said that Dulay is not liable as a principal by indispensable cooperation in the crime of Rape. The Court said all the acts done by Dulay - befriending the girl, bringing the girl in the port area, pushing the girl inside a kubuhan for the girl to be raped by Speed - are not indispensable to consummate the crime of Rape.  
According to the Court, the girl may herself present her body before Speed and still, rape will be committed. The acts done by Dulay can be performed by another person not necessarily Dulay. 
The SC said that Dulay is not a principal by indispensable cooperation. With or without her acts, Speed could still have raped the girl.  The Court said that what Dulay did was an act of procuring child prostitute. Therefore, the liability of Dulay would be a violation of RA 7610 as a procurer of a child prostitute.  
It is evident that the SC looked at the girl as a child prostitute. When the Court said that the girl can present herself to Speed and still, rape will be committed, the Court believes that the girl is a child prostitute who sells her body. Hence, according to the SC, it is not necessary that there be a Dulay to bring her to a customer.  
The SC said that the act done by Dulay is not indispensable in order to consummate the crime of Rape; the liability will be under RA 7610 as a procurer of a child prostitute.

SECTION 1. The penalty of prision correccional in its maximum period, or a fine ranging from 1,000 to 6,000 pesos, or both, shall be imposed upon any person who knowingly or willfully obstructs, impedes, frustrates or delays the apprehension of suspects and the investigation and prosecution of criminal cases by committing any of the following acts:

Q: Is the mother liable as an accessory?  
A: NO. The mother is not liable as an accessory based on the third act for two reasons. First, under the third act of an accessory, if the offender is a private person, the principal must be guilty of Treason, Parricide, Murder, or an Attempt to take the life of the Chief Executive, or is known to be habitually guilty of some other crime. Robbery is not included.  Another reason is, under Art. 20 of the RPC, the mother, being an ascendant of the principal X, the said relationship will exempt her from criminal liability. Under Art. 20, relationship will exempt the accessory from criminal liability if she is related to the principal as a spouse, ascendant, descendant, legitimate, natural, and adopted brother and sister, or a relative by affinity within the same degrees, if the act committed by the accessory pertains to the second and the third acts.  In this case, the act done by the mother pertains to the third act. Therefore, Art. 20, absolutory cause of relationship, applies here. So, for these two reasons, the mother cannot be held liable as an accessory under the third act even if she assisted in the escape of her daughter, the principal.  The mother can, however, be charged for a violation of PD 1829, Obstruction of Justice. In assisting in the escape of her daughter, she frustrated, hampered the arrest of her daughter, and the prosecution and investigation of the crime committed by her daughter.

### ONG v. PEOPLE G.R. NO. 190475 | APRIL 10, 2013 | SERENO, C.J. 
*FACTS:*
Private complainant was the owner of 44 Firestone trucks, and uses chalk to mark them before storing in the warehouse. 6 tires were sold. However, the remaining 38 tires were stolen. Upon checking the area, private complainant chanced upon Jong's Marketing, owned and operated by Ong. Private complainant inquired if Ong was selling any Firestone tires, to which the latter replied in the affirmative. Appellant brought out a tire fitting the description, which private complainant recognized as one of the tires stolen from his warehouse based on the chalk marking and the serial number thereon. Ong was arrested when the police conducted a buy-bust operation on his store. Ong was charged with violation of PD 1612.  
*ISSUE:*
W/N Ong is criminally liable of violation of the Anti-Fencing Law (YES)  
*RULING:*
YES. The essential elements of fencing are as follows: (1) a crime of robbery or theft has been committed; (2) the accused, who is not a principal or on accomplice in the commission of the crime of robbery or theft, buys, receives, possesses, keeps, acquires, conceals, sells or disposes, or buys and sells, or in any manner deals in any article, item, object or anything of value, which has been derived from the proceeds of the crime of robbery or theft; (3) the accused knew or should have known that the said article, item, object or anything of value has been derived from the proceeds of the crime of robbery or theft; and (4) there is, on the part of one accused, intent to gain for oneself or for another.  
In the present case, the SC ruled that the requisites of fencing are present. First, private complainant testified that the crime of robbery had been committed on 17 February 1995. He was able to prove ownership of the tires through Sales Invoice No. 4565 dated 10 November 1994 and an Inventory List. 
Second, although there was no evidence to link Ong as the perpetrator of the robbery, he never denied the fact that 13 tires of the private complainant were caught in his possession. The Court finds that the serial numbers of stolen tires corresponds to those found in Ong's possession.
Third, the accused knew or should have known that the said article, item, object or anything of value has been derived from the proceeds of the crime of robbery or theft. The words "should know" denote the fact that a person of reasonable prudence and intelligence would ascertain the fact in performance of his duty to another or would govern his conduct upon assumption that such fact exists. Ong, who was in the business of buy and sell of tires for the past 24 years, ought to have known the ordinary course of business in purchasing from an unknown seller. Admittedly, Go approached Ong and offered to sell the 13 tires and he did not even ask for proof of ownership of the tires. The entire transaction, from the proposal to buy until the delivery of tires happened in just one day. His experience from the business should have given him doubt as to the legitimate ownership of the tires considering that it was his first time to transact with Go and the manner it was sold is as if Go was just peddling the 13 tires in the streets.  
Finally, there was evident intent to gain for himself, considering that during the buy-bust operation, Ong was actually caught selling the stolen tires in his store, Jong Marketing.  
Further, fencing is malum prohibitum, and P.D. 1612 creates a prima facie presumption of fencing from evidence of possession by the accused of any good, article, item, object or anything of value, which has been the subject of robbery or theft; and prescribes a higher penalty based on the value of the property.

Q: What if the defense raised by the store owner is that he acted in good faith, he lacks criminal intent. Is good faith a defense?
A: NO. In case of Ong v. People, the SC said that good faith or lack of criminal intent is not a valid defense in Fencing because Fencing or violation of PD 1612 is an act malum prohibitum; it is not an act malum inse. Since it is an act malum prohibitum, criminal intent is immaterial. Good faith is not a defense; therefore, the mere fact that the store owner was found in possession of the said jewelries which are the subject of a Robbery, he becomes liable of the offense of Fencing or violation of PD 1612, unless he was able to rebut it by clear and convincing evidence.
